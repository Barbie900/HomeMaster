# Room type: bedroom

Type id: `bedroom`. Dry room. The goals are sleep quality: quiet, dark, fresh air, moderate temperature.

## What makes it different
- **Ventilation overnight** matters: closed windows + two people raise CO₂ quickly. New airtight windows need a ventilation concept.
- **Acoustics and light control** (blinds, black-out), low noise from technical equipment.
- **Temperature** typically 16–19 °C for sleeping, so heating control per room.
- **Fire safety:** smoke detectors, a second escape route on upper floors.

## Decide first
- Where the bed and wardrobes go (power, lights, switches, radiators, windows)
- Built-in wardrobe (floor, plinth, ventilation of the plinth) or freestanding
- Window shading and blinds (box size, power)
- Floor: warm underfoot (parquet, vinyl, cork, carpet)
- Radiator or UFH, a thermostat per room
- Ventilation: trickle vents, mechanical (decentral heat recovery units) or a strict airing routine

## Room-specific rules and numbers
- **Sockets and switches:** bedside sockets on both sides (≈ 30–60 cm above floor or at the bedside table height), a two-way switch at the door and at the bed, a socket for the future cot or desk.
- **Sound:** mineral wool 40–60 mm in partition cavities, solid-core doors with seals, decoupled floor if above another room.
- **Smoke detectors:** in sleeping rooms and escape routes (EN 14604), required by law in many countries. Interconnected wireless units are easy DIY.
- **Escape window on upper floors:** at least one window big enough and low enough to climb out (check local rules for size).
- **Humidity:** target 40–60% RH. Mould on cold exterior corners means a thermal-bridge or ventilation problem. Fix the cause.
- **Wardrobes on exterior walls:** leave a gap or use an insulated back to avoid condensation.

## Extra interview questions
- Who sleeps here (children, light sleepers, shift workers)?
- Street noise side? Ground floor or upper floor? Is the room above or below another bedroom?
- Wardrobe type and size? Desk or dressing table?
- Existing radiators (position, size), window sizes and direction?

## Common mistakes
- No ventilation plan after window replacement.
- Wardrobe against a cold exterior wall without a gap: mould.
- Sockets only on one side of the bed.
- Heating too strong for sleeping, no per-room control.
