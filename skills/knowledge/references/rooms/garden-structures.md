# Room type: garden structures

Type id: `garden-structures`. Shed, pergola, carport, fence, gate, raised beds, retaining wall.

## What makes it different
- **Permit and boundary rules** vary with size, height, roof and distance to the neighbour.
- **Loads:** snow and wind, ground and frost.
- **Underground utilities** and the septic system.
- **Retaining walls and anything people can fall from** are structural: engineer.
- **Electrics outdoors:** licensed electrician.

## Decide first
- What and where: size, height, roof, use (storage, workshop, living, carport)
- Permit or notification (ask the municipality in writing) and neighbour consent
- Foundation type (frost depth) and drainage
- Materials and their durability (timber use class, fixings)
- Power and light, if any

## Task sequence (graph ids)
`survey-and-permits` → `garden-structure-plan` → `site-drainage` → `garden-foundation` → `garden-structure-build` → `electrical-fit-out` (outdoor, licensed)

## Room-specific rules and numbers
See `trades/garden.md`.
- Point footings below the frost line (typically ≈ 80–120 cm), post bases above the ground, slabs 10–15 cm on 15–20 cm gravel.
- Roof pitch ≥ 5–10° for sheet or felt, hold-down straps against uplift, bracing.
- Fences: posts every 1.5–2.5 m, footing depth ≥ 60–100 cm, boundary height limits.
- Retaining walls over ≈ 0.5–1 m: engineer, drainage behind.

## Extra interview questions
- Plot size, boundary distances, slope, soil, trees?
- What goes in the structure? Any stove, workshop tools or power?
- Neighbours and rules (ask if the boundary has a fence already)?
- Where does the rain go? Where are the pipes and cables?

## Common mistakes
- No permit check, building over a pipe, no bracing, no foundation below frost, no drainage behind a retaining wall.
