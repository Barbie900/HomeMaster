# Room type: terrace / patio / balcony

Type id: `terrace`. An outdoor "room" with a floor that has to shed water, survive frost and sun, and not bring moisture into the house.

## What makes it different
- **Water management first:** falls away from the building, drainage, and a surface level below the damp-proof course.
- **Frost and sun:** movement joints, frost-resistant materials, UV-stable products.
- **Substrate type** decides the method (slab, gravel bed, joists). Wrong method for the substrate causes failure.
- **A terrace over a heated room is a roof**, not a patio. That's a different, specialist build-up.
- Size, height and distance to the boundary can require a permit.

## Decide first
- What is below: ground, a concrete slab on ground, or a heated room (roof terrace)?
- Surface: tiles on slab, pavers on gravel, pedestal tiles, wood or composite decking
- Falls and where the water goes (drain, garden, soakaway)
- Threshold from the house door (step-free, drainage at the door)
- Furniture, shade structures (pergola), lighting, outdoor sockets
- Hot tub or outdoor kitchen later (loads, services)

## Task sequence (graph ids)
`survey-and-permits` → `site-drainage` → `outdoor-substrate` → then, by type:
- Tiles on slab: `outdoor-waterproofing` → `outdoor-tiling`
- Pavers on gravel: `paving`
- Decking: `decking`
→ `electrical-fit-out` (outdoor lighting and sockets, by a licensed electrician)

## Room-specific rules and numbers
See `trades/outdoor-surfaces.md` for sub-base, falls, adhesive systems and decking details.
- Falls ≈ 2% for tiles and pavers, ≥ 1% for decking, **away from the house**.
- Finished surface ≥ 15 cm below the damp-proof course (or per local rules), so splash and ponding do not reach the wall.
- Gravel sub-base typically 15–25 cm for foot traffic, thicker for vehicles. Frost-free depth for fixed foundations is region-specific.
- Slab-on-ground tiles: bonded outdoor waterproofing, flexible frost-resistant adhesive, movement joints every ≈ 3 m.

## Extra interview questions
- Existing surface and condition (cracks, falls, standing water)?
- What is under it? Is there a heated room or a cellar below?
- Size, height above ground, distance to the boundary, any roof or pergola planned?
- Orientation and shading, wind, trees (roots, leaves)?
- Door types and thresholds; accessibility?

## Common mistakes
- Falls towards the house.
- Rigid adhesive or non-frost tiles: lifted tiles after winter.
- Decking directly on soil or with no airflow.
- Surface touching the facade or the damp-proof course.
- Building a heated-room terrace like a patio.
