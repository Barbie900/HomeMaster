# Room type: outdoor stairs

Type id: `outdoor-stairs`. Exposed to rain, frost, snow, sun and heavy use. A safety element: falls on stairs are among the commonest household accidents.

## What makes it different
- **Frost and water.** Water that gets in freezes and lifts tiles and cracks concrete.
- **Safety geometry:** equal risers, grip, handrails, and lighting are not options.
- **Foundation** must be frost-free and must not move, or the stairs crack.
- Drop height, setbacks and neighbours may trigger a **permit**.

## Decide first
- Rebuild (new concrete or prefab steps) vs. refurbishing the existing stairs
- Surface: frost-proof porcelain tile, natural stone, concrete with a non-slip finish, wood (rarely)
- Handrail and guard type, with the fixing method (in concrete, not in tiles)
- Drainage of the landing and the steps (falls, drip edge)
- Lighting (step lights, motion sensor)

## Task sequence (graph ids)
`survey-and-permits` → `stairs-structure` (calculate geometry, foundation, formwork and concrete, or existing structure repaired) → `outdoor-waterproofing` → `outdoor-tiling` → `handrail` (anchors sleeved before waterproofing) → `electrical-fit-out` (lighting, by a licensed electrician)

## Room-specific rules and numbers
See `trades/outdoor-stairs.md` for geometry, surfaces, handrails and the adhesive system.
- Riser ≈ 15–18 cm, tread ≈ 28–32 cm. Rule: 2 × riser + tread ≈ 59–65 cm (about 63).
- Same riser and tread over the whole flight. Allowed difference is a few millimetres (≈ 5 mm).
- Surface falls ≈ 1–2% forward so water drains.
- **Guard and handrail** per local rules (see the region file).
- Slip resistance suitable for wet outdoor use (e.g. R11 or the pendulum value in the trade file).

## Extra interview questions
- Number of steps, total rise, available run, width?
- Existing material, condition, any cracks, loose steps, how deep is the foundation?
- Entrance door at the top: how big is the landing, which way does the door open?
- North-facing (ice) or exposed to heavy rain?
- Children, elderly, deliveries (prams, wheelchair, bicycles)? Accessibility ramp needed?

## Common mistakes
- Different riser heights, often after adding tiles to only some steps.
- Non-frost-proof tiles or a rigid adhesive: tiles lift in the first winter.
- Water standing on the steps. No falls or drip edges.
- Handrail fixed into the tiles only.
- No lighting.
