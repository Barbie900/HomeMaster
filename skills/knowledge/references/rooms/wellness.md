# Room type: wellness (sauna, steam room, hot tub, relaxation)

Type id: `wellness`. A heat, humidity and electrical-load room. High consequences for mistakes (fire, scalding, electrical shock, structural overload, mould).

## What makes it different
- **Heat and humidity** are the design loads: materials, vapour barriers and ventilation are chosen for them.
- **Electrical:** high loads (sauna heater 6–9 kW, steam generator, pumps), in wet areas. **A licensed electrician is required.**
- **Fire safety:** clearances to combustibles, heat-rated materials, thermal cut-outs.
- **Weight:** a filled hot tub can weigh 1.5–2.5 t. Check the floor.
- Plumbing, drains and humidity removal for the surrounding rooms.
- A **small indoor pool is a specialist project** (structure, waterproofing, chemistry, dehumidification) and is not covered as DIY here. Plan it with an engineer and a pool builder.

## Decide first
- What: dry sauna, infrared cabin, steam room, hot tub, shower area, relaxation
- Location (ground-floor slab or an upper floor), size, user count
- Electrical supply and the panel capacity (house-wide)
- Ventilation, humidity removal, a floor drain, a shower nearby
- Prefabricated kit vs. built-in construction

## Task sequence (graph ids)
`survey-and-permits` → `wellness-plan` → `panel-and-main-services` (capacity, licensed) → `plumbing-rough-in` → `electrical-rough-in` → `ventilation` → `screed` → `waterproofing` → then either `sauna-build` or `steam-room-build` → `wall-tiling` / `floor-tiling` → `grouting` → `electrical-fit-out` (licensed connection and measurement)

## Room-specific rules and numbers
See `trades/wellness.md`.
- Sauna heater power ≈ 1 kW per m³ of cabin, plus the maker's correction for glass and uninsulated surfaces.
- Steam room: ceiling sloped ≥ 5° (10–15° is better), steam-tight waterproofing on all surfaces.
- Heater, generator and any hot-tub electrical connections: licensed electrician, RCD protection, equipotential bonding.
- **Not DIY:** gas or wood-fired heaters in saunas, the heater's electrical connection, pool structures.

## Extra interview questions
- Which wellness elements and how many people at the same time?
- Electrical capacity: single or three-phase supply, panel size, space?
- Floor type and load rating, ground floor or upper?
- Where could the humid air go? Is there an outside wall for ducting?
- Planned location of the shower and cooling area, and a changing/relaxation area?

## Common mistakes
- Under-sizing the heater or ventilation.
- Insulation placed with no vapour barrier on the warm side: rot.
- Flammable or non-heat-rated cables, lights or foam in the cabin.
- Forgetting the weight of a hot tub.
- Tiled steam room with a flat ceiling: condensate drips on bathers.
