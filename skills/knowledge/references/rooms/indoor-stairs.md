# Room type: indoor stairs

Type id: `indoor-stairs`. A safety element and a structure. The inspection tells you whether to repair, refinish or replace.

## What makes it different
- **Safety geometry:** equal risers, headroom, handrail and guard.
- **Floor levels:** a new floor covering changes the first and last riser. **Recalculate before you change floors.**
- **Escape route:** the stair is often the escape route. Fire doors, smoke detectors and no clutter.
- **Structure:** cutting joists or making a new opening needs an engineer and a permit.

## Decide first
- Inspect, then choose: repair, refinish, cover with a kit, or replace
- The finished floor build-ups upstairs and downstairs (and the total rise)
- Handrail and guard design, lighting and switches
- Whether a new opening or a changed position is needed (engineer)

## Task sequence (graph ids)
`indoor-stairs-inspection` → (floors finished or their thickness known: `floor-covering-dry` / `floor-tiling`) → `indoor-stairs-work` → `painting` → `skirting-and-trim` → `electrical-fit-out` (stair lighting)

## Room-specific rules and numbers
See `trades/stairs-indoor.md`.
- Rise ≈ 17–19 cm, going ≈ 25–30 cm, 2R + T ≈ 59–65 cm, differences between steps ≤ ≈ 5 mm.
- Headroom ≥ ≈ 2.0–2.1 m, width ≥ 80 cm (better 90–100).
- Handrail ≈ 90–100 cm, balusters ≤ 12 cm apart (≤ 10 cm with small children).
- Push test the handrail and the posts.

## Extra interview questions
- Type (straight, L, U, winders, spiral), material, age, number of steps?
- Creaks, loose treads, worn nosings, wobbling handrail?
- Planned changes to floors upstairs and downstairs?
- Children, elderly, deliveries (furniture)? Is the stair an escape route?
- Where does the stair open at the top? Any beams or ceilings that limit the headroom?

## Common mistakes
- Changing floors without recalculating the first and last riser.
- A handrail fixed into plasterboard.
- No light or a missing switch at one end.
- A slippery finish.
