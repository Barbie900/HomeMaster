# Room type: basement / cellar

Type id: `basement`. Below ground, cold surfaces, a high moisture risk. Used as storage, laundry, utility, workshop, or converted to living space.

## What makes it different
- **Moisture decides everything.** Finishing before you know the source wastes the whole job.
- **Condensation** in summer (warm humid air on cold walls) looks like a leak but isn't. The cure is different from lateral water pressure.
- **Radon** (a radioactive soil gas) accumulates in basements in many regions. Measure before and after.
- **Conversion to living space** brings requirements: ceiling height, daylight, ventilation, escape, thermal and damp protection, usually a permit.
- Often holds the boiler, meter and the washing machine.

## Decide first
- Use after the renovation: storage, utility/laundry, workshop, or living space. The latter needs permission and a much higher standard.
- Moisture diagnosis plan (`basement-diagnosis`) before any finishing
- Whether the heating and the hot-water installation stay here
- Exit and escape route, the stair state

`survey-and-permits` → `basement-diagnosis` → `site-drainage` (outside) → `basement-waterproofing` → [`insulation`] → `electrical-rough-in` → `plumbing-rough-in` → [`partitions-and-ceilings`] → `wall-plaster-repair` → [`screed`] → `wall-finish` → `painting` → `floor-tiling` or `floor-covering-dry` → `door-install` → `electrical-fit-out`

## Room-specific rules and numbers
See `trades/basement.md` for diagnosis tests, remedies, ventilation rules and radon.
- Target relative humidity **45–60%**. Keep a hygrometer in the room.
- **Wood, carpet and plasterboard** against masonry below ground are risky. Use mineral materials or treated and ventilated build-ups.
- **Electrical:** RCD-protected circuits, sockets above any flood level, IP-rated fittings in damp rooms.
- **Flood risk:** don't store valuables or electrics low. Consider a sump pump with a battery backup and a non-return valve on the drain.

## Extra interview questions
- Where does it get damp, in which seasons, how high up the wall, any salt deposits or musty smell?
- Gutters and downpipes: where do they discharge? Ground sloping towards the house?
- Wall construction and age (stone, brick, concrete), any exterior waterproofing?
- Floor: concrete slab, earth, with or without a membrane?
- Radon measured? Cracks growing?
- Planned use as living space?

## Common mistakes
- Painting or tiling over damp walls.
- Interior insulation on a wall that is taking in water.
- Ventilating on hot humid days, which brings in more moisture.
- Ignoring gutters and ground levels.
- A "dehumidifier fix" with no diagnosis.
