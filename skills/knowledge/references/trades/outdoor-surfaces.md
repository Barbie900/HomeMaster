# Trade: Outdoor surfaces (drainage, terrace, paving, decking)

Graph tasks: `site-drainage`, `outdoor-substrate`, `outdoor-waterproofing`, `outdoor-tiling`, `paving`, `decking`. For stairs see `outdoor-stairs.md`. Typical values: the **product data sheets** and **local frost depth and permit rules** win.

**DIY level:** gravel-bed paving and decking are very good DIY projects (hard physical work). Tiling a slab outdoors is DIY for experienced tilers, but it's less forgiving than indoors. **Not DIY / get a professional:** terraces above heated rooms (roof build-ups), new foundations for large structures, retaining walls over ≈ 1 m, and anything that changes how the rainwater leaves the plot.

## Before anything: water and ground (task: `site-drainage`)
- **Where does the water go?** Falls ≥ 2% away from the building, ideally 5% over the first 2 m on the garden side. A terrace must never drain towards the wall.
- **Damp-proof course (DPC):** keep the finished surface **≥ 15 cm below it** (or per local rules), and keep soil, mulch and paving from touching the facade.
- **Rainwater:** connect downpipes to a permitted discharge (sewer, soakaway, a watercourse). A soakaway needs suitable soil, a safe distance from the building (typically ≥ 3–5 m) and often a permit.
- **Sleeves first:** lay **empty pipes (Ø50–110 mm, with pull cords, ends marked)** under every future surface for **irrigation, power, lighting and data**. Cables and pipes are cheap before the surface and ruinous after (see `irrigation.md`, `landscaping.md`).
- **Call before you dig:** locate gas, water, electricity and communications lines. Hand-dig near them.
- **Frost depth:** fixed foundations (posts, walls, stairs) go **below the frost line**, typically **≈ 80–120 cm in central Europe**. Check the municipality.
- **Permits:** area, height above ground, roofing and distance to the boundary can require a permit. Check the region file.

## Choose the build-up
| Type | Substrate | Good for | Cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pavers/slabs on a gravel bed** | compacted sub-base | most gardens, natural drainage | needs edge restraint, settling if the sub-base is poor |
| **Tiles on a concrete slab** | existing sound slab with falls | modern look, sealed surfaces, **slab above a cellar** | waterproofing, drainage, movement joints, frost-resistant tiles and adhesive |
| **Pedestal pavers (adjustable supports)** | slab or flat roof, drained | levelling, accessing drains, over membranes | height, wind loads, cost |
| **Wood/composite decking** | joists on pads or a grid | warm underfoot, quick | ventilation, rot, board gaps, UV |
**Recommendation:** for a garden terrace on soil: **gravel-bed paving** (simplest, most forgiving). For a terrace on an existing slab above a cellar or next to doors: **tiles on a slab with waterproofing, or pedestal pavers**. For quick, warm and light: **decking on a ventilated joist grid**.

## Build: paving on a gravel bed (task: `paving`)
1. **Excavate** to the total build-up depth: sub-base 15–25 cm (foot traffic), + 3–5 cm bedding + paver thickness (≥ 4–6 cm). **Strip topsoil.** Set string lines with falls ≈ 2%.
2. **Geotextile** on soft or clay soils. **Sub-base:** crushed stone (e.g. 0/32 or 0/45), **in layers ≤ 10–15 cm, compacted with a plate compactor (rent)**.
3. **Edge restraint:** concrete haunching, kerbs or a metal edge.
4. **Bedding:** 3–5 cm of **grit/chippings** (e.g. 2/5 or 1/3 mm) **screeded flat** with rails. **Don't compact the bedding before the pavers.**
5. **Lay the pavers** with 3–5 mm joints, working from the edge or a straight line, **walk on the laid pavers only, not on the bedding**. Cut with a wet saw or a block splitter.
6. **Compact** with a plate compactor and a rubber mat, **fill the joints** with kiln-dried sand or the proper jointing compound, compact again, and top up.
7. **Check the falls** with water or a level.

## Build: tiles on a slab (tasks: `outdoor-waterproofing`, `outdoor-tiling`)
1. **Slab** must be ≥ 28 days old, sound, with falls ≈ 2% and drainage outlets. Fix cracks and bumps. **Anchors for posts and railings** are fitted and sleeved now.
2. **Bonded waterproofing** (a system rated for outdoor use and frost, with tape in corners and collars on drains) is applied to the slab, with the edges, drip profiles and door thresholds.
3. **Drainage layer:** a drainage mat or a gravel/pedestal solution under the tiles so the water can leave. **A water-tight sandwich with no exit freezes and lifts the tiles.**
4. **Tiles:** frost-resistant porcelain with a very low water absorption (typically ≤ 0.5%, tested for frost) and a slip-resistance suitable for wet outdoor use (e.g. **R11** or a pendulum value ≥ 36). Thickness 20 mm for gravel/pedestal use, 8–12 mm for a bonded slab (per the maker).
5. **Adhesive:** a **flexible, frost-resistant C2 S1 or S2** (as the system data sheet says), applied with the **floating and buttering method** for **full contact** (no hollows where water can freeze).
6. **Movement joints:** perimeter and in fields of about **9 m² (≈ 3 × 3 m)**, over the joints of the slab, at every change of plane. **Joint widths ≈ 5 mm or more.** Flexible, frost-resistant grout, and a sealant for the movement joints.
7. **Temperatures:** work between +5 and +30 °C with **no rain or frost until the adhesive and grout have cured** (typically 48–72 h for the cover, a week or more for full cure). Cover the area with tarpaulins and let the air flow under.
8. **Drip edges and falls** at the borders so the water doesn't run back onto the facade.

## Build: decking (task: `decking`)
1. **Substrate:** compacted gravel or slab with falls and drainage. **Joists** (hardwood, treated softwood, aluminium, or WPC system joists) on **pads or adjustable supports**, **never on bare soil**, with **≥ 5–10 cm airflow** underneath and no standing water.
2. **Joist spacing:** typically **40–50 cm** for 21–28 mm timber boards, **30–40 cm** for composite (per the maker), a **double joist** where boards meet. Slope the joists ≥ 1% along the water path.
3. **Boards:** gap **5–8 mm** between boards (timber needs it to swell and drain), **gaps at the ends and walls**, fixed with stainless steel screws (pre-drilled in hardwood), or hidden clips. **Seal the cut ends.**
4. **Edges:** don't let boards touch the facade. Provide access hatches for cleaning and for drains.
5. **Timber maintenance:** oil or let it weather. Composite expands in heat. Follow the maker's expansion gaps.

## Tools
Plate compactor (rent), wet saw or block splitter, long straightedge/rails, string line, laser or hose level, rubber mallet, wheelbarrow, spade and shovel, a rake, a tamper, a diamond blade, notched trowels, cement mixer, mixing paddles, flexible joint tools, a drill/driver for decking, safety gear (gloves, glasses, FFP3 for cutting, ear protection, safety boots).

## Common mistakes
- Falls towards the house or none.
- Surface touching or above the damp-proof course.
- Rigid adhesive or porous tiles: lifted tiles after the first winter.
- No movement joints.
- A thin or uncompacted sub-base: settling, ruts.
- Decking directly on soil, or without gaps, or with a wrong joist spacing.
- Ignoring utilities before digging.

## Quality checks
- **Falls** checked with a water test after rain or hose. No puddles after 30 min.
- Flatness ≈ 3–4 mm under a 2 m straightedge (outdoor tolerance), joints even.
- Tap test and lift test of a few tiles: ≥ 90% contact, no hollows.

## Cross-trade
- Basement (drainage, grading), doors and thresholds (levels), electrical (outdoor-rated, RCD, installed by a licensed electrician), stairs (shared drainage and surfaces), permits.

## Element: excavation and sub-base
1. **Mark** the outline with pegs and string, **mark the finished level and the falls** (≈ 2%) on the pegs, **locate the utilities**.
2. **Excavate** to the **total build-up depth** (sub-base 15–25 cm + bedding 3–5 cm + paver). **Strip topsoil**, **remove soft spots**.
3. **Geotextile** on soft or clay ground. **Sub-base:** crushed stone **0/32 or 0/45** in layers of **≤ 10–15 cm**, **compacted with a plate compactor** (typically 3–4 passes per layer).
4. **Check** the levels and falls with a string and a spirit level **before** adding the bedding.

## Element: edge restraint
1. **Concrete haunching** (≈ 10 cm × 15 cm) on the outside, or **kerbs, a metal edge**, or **a wall**, **set to the finished level**.
2. **Curing** (≥ 2–3 days) before the plate compactor touches it.
3. **Corner and curve edges** as a design detail. **Don't skip** this: **paving without an edge spreads**.

## Element: bedding and laying pavers
1. **Bedding:** **3–5 cm of grit or chippings** (2/5 or 1/3 mm), **screeded flat** over **rails**, **never compacted before laying**. **Don't walk on it.**
2. **Lay** from a **straight edge or a corner**, **joints 3–5 mm**, **pavers pressed in with a rubber mallet**, **string line** every few rows, **work from the laid surface**.
3. **Cuts** with a **wet saw or a block splitter**, **at the edges**, **FFP3 mask**.
4. **Falls** checked every row.

## Element: joints and final compaction
1. **Sweep kiln-dried joint sand** (or the jointing compound) into the dry joints.
2. **Compact** with a plate compactor and a **rubber mat**, two passes.
3. **Top up** the joints with sand and compact again. **Re-sand after the first rains** and in the first season.

## Element: slab waterproofing
1. **Slab**: ≥ 28 days old, sound, **falls ≈ 2%**, **drains at the low points**, **no depressions**.
2. **Prime**, **tape** at wall-floor corners and **around drains** with sealing tape and collars **of one system**.
3. **Two coats** of the **outdoor-rated, frost-resistant membrane** (data sheet thickness), crossing directions. **Cure** protected from rain (typically 24–48 h or the sheet's value).
4. **Edges and thresholds:** the membrane **up the wall ≥ 15 cm** and **over the threshold** where the system says. **Drip profiles** at free edges.

## Element: tiles on the slab and joints
1. **Frost-resistant porcelain** (water absorption ≤ 0.5%), **slip resistance R11** (wet), **20 mm tiles** on gravel or pedestals, **8–12 mm** bonded to a slab.
2. **Adhesive:** **flexible, frost-resistant C2 S1 or S2**, **floating-and-buttering method** (a comb on the slab and a flat coat on the tile) for **full contact**.
3. **Layout:** **joints ≥ 4–5 mm**, **movement joints** at the perimeter and every **≈ 3 m (≈ 9 m² fields)**, over the slab's joints.
4. **Grout:** **flexible, frost-resistant** (CG2WA, or epoxy), **movement joints with a sealant**.
5. **Weather:** +5 to +30 °C, **no rain or frost for 48–72 h**, **cover** the area, **keep off foot traffic for several days**.

## Element: joists and supports (decking)
1. **Level the substrate** (gravel or slab) with **falls ≥ 1%** along the water path. **Supports:** **pads or adjustable pedestals**, **never bare soil**, **≥ 5–10 cm airflow** below.
2. **Joists:** hardwood, treated softwood, aluminium or a composite system, **spacing 40–50 cm for 21–28 mm timber boards**, **30–40 cm for composite** (per the maker). **A double joist where boards meet.**
3. **Plastic shims or a membrane** between the joist and the support. **Keep joists off standing water.**
4. **Check** the level, flatness and square before the boards.

## Element: boards, fixing and edges (decking)
1. **Gaps:** **5–8 mm between boards** (wood), the composite maker's value, **gaps at the ends and walls**.
2. **Fixing:** **stainless steel screws** (pre-drilled in hardwood) or **hidden clips**. **Two fixings per joist** per board.
3. **Cut ends** sealed, **edges** finished with **a fascia board**, **no boards touching the facade**.
4. **Maintenance:** **oil** or let it weather, **clean** annually, **re-tighten** after the first season.

## Element: drains and outlets
1. **Channel drains** at the doors and where the falls meet, **connected to a permitted discharge** (`site-drainage`).
2. **Falls** to the drain ≥ 1–2%, **the grating level with the surface**, **a trap or a silt box** to catch debris.
3. **Clean** before autumn and after storms.
