# Trade: Waterproofing (bonded membranes under tile)

Graph tasks: `waterproofing`. Standards: EN 14891 (liquid-applied membranes under tile), EN 12004 (adhesives). The **system maker's data sheet** gives all thicknesses, times and primers.

**DIY level:** very much DIY, if you follow the system strictly and **don't skip steps**. This is the step that decides whether the bathroom stays dry for 20 years. Everything is about **continuity**: tape in every corner, collars on every pipe, overlap at every joint.

## Which system? (comparison)
| System | Pros | Cons | DIY |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Liquid membrane, polymer dispersion ("liquid foil", ready to use)** | easy to apply, thin, fast. Good on walls | needs a dry substrate (screed must pass CM). Needs 2 coats with exact dry thickness | ★★★ |
| **Flexible cement slurry (2-component, CM-type)** | tolerates a little moisture. Good crack-bridging. Cheap | thicker, needs more drying time | ★★ |
| **Sheet membrane (PE/fleece) bonded with adhesive** | uniform thickness guaranteed, can be tiled the next day | cutting and sealing corners/penetrations is slower | ★★ |
| **Bonded waterproof boards/panels (XPS-based)** | build the shower, niche and bench quickly, built-in waterproof | more expensive. Needs sealed joints and tape | ★★★ |
**Recommendation for DIY (tiled shower, drywall or plaster walls, screed floor):** liquid membrane + sealing tape + collars from **one system**, with **bonded waterproof panels for niches and benches**. Use the same maker's primer and tile adhesive.

## Coverage (typical; follow your regional rules)
- **Shower:** the whole floor + walls from the floor to ≥ 200 cm (or above the shower head).
- **Bath surround:** walls ≥ 15–20 cm above the rim and the whole splash zone.
- **Basin zone and other walls:** at least up to the splash height (≈ 120–150 cm) at the basin; the **entire bathroom floor** is recommended, with a 15–20 cm upturn.
- **Door threshold:** carry the membrane over the threshold and ≥ 10 cm beyond.
- **Window reveals and niches:** all faces.

## Materials (one system)
Primer, membrane (liquid or sheet), **sealing tape (corners, wall–floor, wall–wall)**, **pre-formed corners**, **drain collar or fleece flange** matching the shower drain, **pipe collars** (for each tap, shower, WC frame penetration), movement-joint tape, bonded panels for niches. For quantities: `quantities.md`.

## Tools
- Roller (short-pile) and a brush (flat, 80–100 mm), mixing paddle, clean buckets
- Wet-film thickness comb (cheap, rent/buy: confirms coat thickness)
- Scissors, knife, a moisture meter for the walls and a **CM measurement for screed**
- Don't speed up curing with heaters or a hot-air gun unless the maker allows it

## Method
1. **Check the substrate:** sound, clean, dry, flat. Screed has **passed the CM test**. Plaster is cured and dry. Remove dust (vacuum, not broom).
2. **Prime** (as the maker says, usually dilute first coat; wait for it to dry).
3. **Fill the corners first:** bed the **sealing tape** into the first membrane coat at every wall–floor and wall–wall junction in the wet zones, and the movement-joint tape on joints. Press out air bubbles, overlap the tape ≥ 5 cm at the joins.
4. **Drain flange:** bed the flange/fleece collar into the membrane without folds. The sealing collar must be tight against the drain body.
5. **Pipe penetrations:** slide the collars over the pipes, bed them in the membrane, no folds.
6. **First coat**: brush the corners and edges; roll the surfaces. **Dry per data sheet** (typically 2–4 h; check by touch and not tacky).
7. **Second coat**, crossing direction (first horizontal, second vertical). Check **no pinholes** against light. Total dry film: per system (commonly 1–2 mm).
8. **Wet-film check** with a comb while applying: that is how you prove the thickness.
9. **Cure** (typical 12–24 h before tiling), keeping the room dry and free of draughts, without direct heat. Don't walk on it with dirty shoes.
10. **Flood test** of the shower floor (optional but strongly recommended): block the drain, fill to ≈ 2 cm, mark the water line, and wait **24 h**. Check below for leaks and the level. In an apartment block, tell the neighbours below first.

## Tips & tricks
- Photograph the membrane with the sealing tape and collars before tiling. The photos **document it for insurers and future owners**.
- Mix small batches. Keep the pot lid closed (dispersions skin over).
- Press the membrane **up the wall** (min. 15–20 cm) around the floor perimeter. The wall's own waterproofing laps over it.
- Tape every drywall joint and screw head in the wet zones before the membrane.

## Common mistakes
- Only the shower floor waterproofed, with bare drywall behind the tiled walls.
- No tape at the corners, or tape not lapped: the corners always leak first.
- Mixing systems (primer of one maker, membrane of another).
- Applying onto damp screed: blisters and no bond.
- Too thin: counting coats, not measuring.
- Drilling through the membrane later for glass fixings without sealing (use sealant, use sleeves).

## Quality checks
- Every corner taped, collars sealed, no pinholes or fold-overs.
- Wet-film comb readings OK at several places, total consumption matches the quantity calculated.
- Flood test passed (water level didn't drop beyond ≈ 1–2 mm).

## Cross-trade
- Plumbing: drain collar height and pipe collars **before** the membrane.
- Screed: CM test and primed.
- Drywall: board type and sealed joints.
- Tiling: use the adhesive class the system maker approves (usually C2TE S1) and the same maker's grout/silicone if possible.
- Electrical: boxes in wet walls need the membrane taped around them.
