# Trade: Carpentry (bathroom door and frame)

Graph tasks: `door-install`.

**DIY level:** DIY with a helper. The key is **height and ventilation**, not carpentry skill.

## Decide first
- **Finished floor level (FFL)** at the doorway: the new floor build-up changes the clearance under the door. Measure from the **top of the finished tile**, not from the old floor.
- Door type for a bathroom: **moisture-resistant** door and frame (HPL or painted/sealed wood, with sealed bottom edge). Avoid untreated MDF at the bottom edge.
- Opening direction: inward doors swing over the floor. Check **clearance for the new threshold and tiles**. Outward-opening doors save space but need a safe hallway.
- **Ventilation:** undercut ≈ 1–1.5 cm under the door or a grille (replacement air for the extract fan).
- A **transition profile** between the tile and the hallway floor if the heights differ.

## Method
1. **Order:** hang the door **after tiling** (frame sits on the finished floor) or set the frame **to FFL** before tiling. Don't tile around an unfinished frame without protecting it.
2. Set the frame plumb in both planes with a level, wedge it, and fix it per type (screw through shims, foam only where the maker allows. **Foam can bow frames**).
3. Seal the bottom of the frame with silicone where it meets the tile. **Seal the bottom edge of the door** (all four edges).
4. Hang the door, check clearance (≈ 3–4 mm sides, ≈ 10–15 mm bottom).
5. Fit the handle/lock; the transition profile or threshold.

## Tips
- If the door needs shortening, **seal the cut edge** immediately.
- Take the door off the hinges during dusty work or tiling and store it flat.

## Common mistakes
- Door no longer clears the new floor.
- Frame in a wet corner fixed with untreated wood.
- No make-up air route for the extractor.

## Cross-trade
- Screed and floor build-up (finished level), tiling (threshold), ventilation (undercut).

## Element: interior door and frame
1. **Measure the opening** (width, height, wall thickness, plumb) and the **floor build-up** of both rooms. **Order the door** to fit the frame (**leaf + ≈ 3–4 mm per side**, ≈ 10–15 mm at the bottom, ≈ 10 mm over a wet-room floor or with extraction).
2. **Frame types:** a **block frame** (fixed with screws and shims into the masonry), a **wrap-around frame** (a lining, glued or clipped), **a hidden frame**. Pick the one matching the wall thickness.
3. **Set the frame** plumb in both directions, wedge it at the hinge and lock points (**3 hinge points, ≈ 20–30 cm from the ends**), **fix through the shims**. Foam only with low-expansion foam and spacers that keep the frame straight.
4. **Hang the door:** **3 hinges** (heavy doors, 4), a **3–4 mm even gap** at the sides and the top, the lock and the **strike plate** aligned. **Test** that it stays open and closed in any position (no self-closing by tilt).
5. **Seal** the cut edges of any trimmed leaf. **Wet rooms:** a **moisture-resistant door** with a sealed bottom edge.
6. **Finish:** casing or architrave, **doorstops**, then the **paint touch-up** (`painting.md`).

## Element: skirting boards
1. **After** the floor and the painted walls. **Measure** the walls and plan **joints away from the eye** (a long board in the view).
2. **Cut the corners:** **inside corners scribed (coped)** or mitred at 45°, **outside corners mitred**. **Dry-fit** each piece.
3. **Fix to the wall (never to the floor):** **assembly adhesive and a few finishing nails or screws** at about 40–60 cm. **A gap of ≈ 1–2 mm above a floating floor**, so it can move under it.
4. **Fill and paint:** nail holes filled, a **flexible joint** (acrylic caulk) along the wall, **finish coat** (`painting.md`).
5. **Cable skirting** for network and low-voltage cables if you want them hidden.

## Element: built-in wardrobe
1. **Measure** the niche at 3 heights and the floor. **Levelling the plinth** with a laser or spirit level.
2. **Fix the frame** to the wall with **plugs suited to the wall** (anti-tilt fixing to the wall at the top), **never rely on the floor** alone. **Allow** for skirting and the ceiling.
3. **Ventilation:** a gap behind the back (**≈ 2 cm, or the wall's insulation if it is an outside wall**) and vents at the plinth. **Never against a cold outside wall without an air gap or an insulated back.**
4. **Doors:** a **sliding track** perfectly level, hinged doors adjusted with a 2–3 mm even gap.
5. **Power and light** (an LED strip inside, **low-voltage**, with a sensor), the cable routed **before** the wardrobe is closed.
6. **Load:** shelves ≈ 20–30 kg/m, **heavy items on the lower shelves**.

## Element: threshold and mat well
1. **Mat well:** a **recessed frame in the screed** (≈ 1–2 cm), **≥ 80 cm along the walking line**, flush with the finished floor, with a **drain** or a way to clean it. **Plan it before the screed**.
2. **Door threshold:** a **sealed, low threshold** (≤ 2 cm where accessibility matters), **slope to the outside**, a **drip edge**, and the **weather seal** of the door pressed evenly.
3. **Floors on both sides at the same level** wherever possible, a bevelled profile where not.

## Element: shoe and coat storage
1. **Wall backing** for the coat rail and heavy shelves. **Height** of the rail ≈ 160–170 cm, hooks for children ≈ 100–120 cm.
2. **A dedicated, ventilated zone** for wet shoes (a grille, a tray, a bench with a slatted shelf).
3. **Easy-clean wall protection** to ≈ 120–150 cm (washable paint or a panel).
4. **Don't block** the electrical panel (≥ 80 cm in front) or the escape route.
