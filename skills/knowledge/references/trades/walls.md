# Trade: Walls and ceilings (plaster repair, chases, painting)

Graph tasks: `wall-plaster-repair`, `ceiling-finish`.

**DIY level:** DIY. Plaster repairs need patience (drying time), not special skills.

## Wet-area plaster
- **Never gypsum plaster in wet zones.** Use **cement or lime-cement** plaster/render (or repair mortar), then waterproof and tile.
- Walls to be tiled need flatness ≤ 3 mm over 2 m: a smooth finish is not needed. A **rough, flat** surface grips better.
- **Chase filling:** cement-based repair mortar, **with mesh** over wide chases and where materials change (brick/plaster, pipes/plaster). Overfill slightly and scrape flat.
- **Hollow or crumbling plaster:** remove completely to sound background (tap test). Primer, then re-plaster.

## Method: repair
1. **Prepare:** vacuum dust. Wet the chase (or prime with the bonding primer), because dry masonry sucks the water out of the mortar.
2. **Fill** in layers (max ≈ 15–20 mm per layer), mesh bedded in the first layer. Compact.
3. **Level** with a straight edge, float the surface when it begins to stiffen. Scratch the surface lightly if another layer follows.
4. **Dry:** typically ≈ **1 day per mm** of thickness for cement/lime-cement (up to the data sheet). Before waterproofing, compare a moisture-meter reading with a known dry wall in the same building, and follow the system maker's limit. For a reliable value use a CM measurement.
5. **Check flatness** with the 2 m straightedge. Fix deviations > 3 mm.

## Primer and damp
- **Active damp or salts** (efflorescence): find and fix the cause (leaks, rising damp, condensation) *before* any waterproofing and tile.
- Use the same maker's **primer** the waterproofing needs.

## Ceilings and painting
- Ceiling in a bathroom: **moisture-resistant paint** (high scrub class, anti-mould additive), with **primer first**. Paint **before** tiling the walls so drips don't hit tiles, and mask the ceiling line on the walls after.
- Make sure the **ventilation** is operational before painting the ceiling.
- Use fine-grit sanding and filler **only** for dry zones.

## Tools
- Plasterer's trowel and float, hawk, level and 2 m straightedge, mixing paddle, mesh and primer brush
- Moisture meter (good for relative comparisons, not for CM)
- Roller (short pile), brush, ladder or a platform, drop cloths

## Common mistakes
- Gypsum filler in the wet zone.
- Filling a chase in one thick pass: shrinkage and cracks.
- Waterproofing over damp plaster: blisters.
- Painting before the extractor is installed, mould appears.

## Quality checks
- Flatness ≤ 3 mm / 2 m, surface sound and dust-free.
- Moisture in the plaster at the data-sheet level.

## Cross-trade
- Plumbing tested and photographed before closing chases.
- Electrical boxes aligned to the future wall surface.
- Waterproofing and tiling need the flat, dry, primed surface.
