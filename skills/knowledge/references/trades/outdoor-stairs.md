# Trade: Outdoor stairs (geometry, structure, surface, handrail)

Graph tasks: `stairs-structure`, `handrail` (with `outdoor-waterproofing`, `outdoor-tiling` in `outdoor-surfaces.md`). Local building rules (guard heights, handrails, permits) vary: always check the region file and municipality.

**DIY level:** refurbishing existing concrete stairs (repair, waterproofing, tiling, handrails) is DIY for a careful person. **New stairs, stairs on poor ground, or any change in the foundation: have an engineer check the design.** Falls on stairs are a leading cause of household injuries, so geometry and railings are not optional.

## Geometry (typical, check local rules)
- **Riser (rise) R ≈ 15–18 cm**, **tread (going) T ≈ 28–32 cm**. Comfort rule: **2R + T ≈ 59–65 cm** (≈ 63 cm).
- **Equal risers and treads across the whole flight.** The allowed difference is small (≈ 5 mm). Plan the **finished surfaces** into the calculation (tile + adhesive thickness) so the *finished* risers are equal, **including the first and the last step**.
- **Number of steps:** n = total rise ÷ R (round), then R = rise ÷ n. Example: total rise 85 cm → n = 5 steps with R = 17 cm → T ≈ 63 − 34 = 29 cm.
- **Width:** ≥ 90–100 cm (≥ 120 cm for comfort).
- **Landing:** at doors, at least as deep as the door leaf and ≥ 100 cm, with a level surface and a slight fall (≈ 1–2%) away from the door.
- **Nosing overhang** ≤ 3 cm. A **drip edge** so water doesn't run over the face.
- **Surface fall:** ≈ 1–2% forward on each tread so water leaves.

## Structure and foundation
- **Cast concrete stairs** on a **frost-free foundation** (below the frost line, typically ≈ 80–120 cm) tied into the building properly (or isolated as the design says), reinforced by an engineer's design. Prefabricated steps are an option on a well-compacted gravel base, but they can settle.
- **Existing stairs:** inspect for cracks, loose steps, hollow sound, rusting rebars, damp, and slope. Repair with repair mortar (and a bonding primer), re-form the profile, and wait for the cure before waterproofing. **Moving cracks = structural problem.**

## Surfaces
- **Frost-resistant porcelain tiles** with a **very low water absorption (typically ≤ 0.5%)**. Surface slip resistance suitable for wet outdoor use (**R11**, or a pendulum value ≥ 36), with an edge profile or a tile with an integral nosing.
- **Adhesive:** flexible, frost-resistant **C2 S1 or S2**, **floating and buttering method (100% contact)**. **Grout:** flexible, frost-resistant, with **movement joints at every step edge**, between treads and risers, and every ≈ 2–3 m on long landings.
- **Waterproofing:** a bonded outdoor system on the whole stair and landing, with falls and drip profiles. **Never trap water under tiles.**
- **Natural stone and concrete treatments** need non-slip finishes (rough, brushed, flamed).
- **Anti-slip strips** and contrast nosings are good for seniors, rain and ice.

## Handrail and guard (task: `handrail`)
- **Handrail** where there are **≥ 3–4 steps** or the rule says (check the region), on **at least one side** (both sides for width ≥ 120 cm or for older users). **Height 90–100 cm** measured over the nosing line, **grip diameter 40–50 mm**, continuous, extended slightly beyond the first and last step, and returning to the wall or post to avoid snagging.
- **Guard** where the **drop at an open side exceeds roughly 50–100 cm** (check local rules), height ≥ 90–100 cm (≥ 110 cm at larger heights), **openings ≤ 12 cm (≤ 10 cm where small children are expected)**, and **no horizontal rails that can be climbed**.
- **Fixings:** into solid concrete or masonry with the right anchors (chemical anchors for the most critical posts), not only into tiles or render. **Plan the anchor and sleeve positions before the waterproofing and tiling**. Seal the sleeves with the system sealant. **Test** with a firm lateral load before use.
- **Material:** hot-dip galvanised or stainless steel, or treated hardwood.

## Lighting and details
- **Step lights or low bollards** on a separate outdoor circuit with RCD (**installed by a licensed electrician**). Motion or twilight sensors.
- **Drainage:** a channel or drain at the foot of the stairs and at the doorstep. **Snow and ice:** avoid north-facing surfaces without heating or plan for grit.
- **Contrast** on nosings (a lighter or darker strip) for visibility.

## Method (refurbish and tile existing concrete stairs)
1. **Measure each step** (rise and going) and calculate the **finished** geometry with the planned surface thickness. If risers differ more than ≈ 5 mm, **correct them with repair mortar or a levelling screed** before the surface.
2. **Repair and prepare** the substrate (clean, sound, falls). **Drill and sleeve** the handrail anchor points.
3. **Prime and waterproof** (bonded system, tapes, collars, drip profiles). Cure per the data sheet, protected from rain.
4. **Tile** from the **top down** (or the bottom up, per the design), treads and risers with the floating-buttering method. Fix the **nosing profiles**. Check with a level and a straight edge: the **falls and equal risers**.
5. **Grout and movement joints** (flexible), after 24–72 h at suitable temperatures.
6. **Handrail** fixed, tested and **lighting** installed.

## Tools
Tape measure, level, laser, a straightedge, bonding primer and repair mortar, trowels, a wet saw or an angle grinder with a diamond blade, anchor drill (SDS), hole saw, mixing paddle, a tile-spacer set, cutting guide, sealant and a gun, safety gear.

## Common mistakes
- Risers that differ (tiled steps with different adhesive thickness, the first and last step not matching).
- Rigid adhesive, porous tiles, no movement joints, and trapped water.
- No falls, water standing on treads.
- Handrail fixed into tiles only, or at the wrong height.
- Handrail anchors drilled after waterproofing (leaks).
- Lighting forgotten.

## Quality checks
- Risers within ≈ 5 mm across the whole flight. Falls checked with water.
- Handrail load test, no wobble. Openings and height meet the rule.
- No hollow-sounding tiles (tap test), no cracks in the first winter.

## Element: foundation and concrete steps (new stairs)
**An engineer designs it.** The points below are for understanding.
1. **Frost-free footing** (below the frost line, typically ≈ 80–120 cm), on **undisturbed or compacted ground**, with **drainage** around.
2. **Formwork** to the calculated riser and going, **reinforcement** as designed, **cover to the steel** per the design (≈ 3–4 cm outdoors).
3. **Concrete** of the **frost and de-icing class** the design says, **compacted**, **cured moist for ≥ 7 days** (cover and mist).
4. **Falls** ≈ 1–2% forward on each step, and **drip edges** cast in.
5. **Wait 28 days** before waterproofing and tiling (or the maker's moisture value).

## Element: repairing and levelling existing steps
1. **Measure** every riser and going. **Differences over ≈ 5 mm** must be corrected.
2. **Remove** loose parts, **hammer-test** for hollow spots, **expose rusty steel**, treat and cover.
3. **Prime** with a bonding primer, **build up** with **repair mortar** (thin layers) to the planned finished geometry **including the tile thickness**.
4. **Falls:** ≈ 1–2% forward, no pooling. **Cure** the repair mortar and **check again** before the next layer.
5. **Moving cracks:** structural, **engineer**, don't hide them.

## Element: nosings, drip edges and joints
1. **Nosing:** a **profile with a drip edge** or an integrated non-slip tile edge, **fixed in the adhesive** and **sealed**.
2. **Drip edge** so water drops off the face and not along the riser.
3. **Movement joints** at each **step edge and between treads and risers**, a **flexible, frost-resistant joint**, **not rigid grout**.
4. **Contrast** on the nosing (a lighter or darker strip) and **anti-slip strips** if the surface is smooth.

## Element: step lighting and drainage
1. **Lighting:** low-voltage LED step lights or bollards, **a separate outdoor circuit with RCD** (licensed electrician), **a switch or a twilight or motion sensor**, **conduit** set before the tiling.
2. **Drainage:** a **channel** or a **drain** at the foot and at the door sill, **falls** away from the house, **no water running under the door**.
3. **Winter:** salt-safe materials, **grit**, and **no south-facing ice traps** without a plan.
