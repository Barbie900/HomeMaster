# Trade: Ventilation (bathroom extract)

Graph tasks: `ventilation`, links to `ceiling-finish`. Typical values; check fan data sheets and local rules.

**DIY level:** DIY for ducting and fan mounting. The electrical connection of the fan follows the electrical rules (licensed electrician where required).

## What it must do
A bathroom produces ≈ 1–2 l of water vapour per shower hour per person. Without extraction: mould, peeling paint, damp walls.
- **Windowless bathroom:** mechanical extraction is required.
- **With a window:** an extractor is still strongly recommended. Winter window ventilation isn't enough.

## Sizing
- Typical bathroom fan: **60–100 m³/h** (≈ 15–30 l/s), run-on timer or humidity sensor.
- Duct: rigid smooth pipe Ø100 mm (or Ø125) is better than flex. Every 90° bend and every metre loses airflow, so keep the duct short (≤ 5–6 m) with few bends.
- **Replacement air:** must be able to flow in: door undercut ≈ 1–1.5 cm or a transfer grille. Without it the fan labours and makes noise.

## Materials
- Axial or centrifugal fan (centrifugal for long ducts) with a **backdraft damper**
- Duct, connectors, clips, aluminium tape
- Insulation for ducts in cold areas (attic, unheated rooms): condensation drips back otherwise
- Exterior hood with flaps and an insect screen
- Fire/acoustic considerations per local rules (apartment blocks often have a shared shaft with its own rules)

## Tools
- Core drill (≈ Ø100–125 mm) or a diamond core bit for exterior wall penetrations (rent)
- Level, hole saw for plasterboard, silicone and sealant, foam
- Sealing tape

## Method
1. **Check the route:** to an outside wall, roof or the building's ventilation shaft **only if permitted**. Never discharge into an attic, a cavity, a chimney or another room. In apartment blocks, ask whether there is a dedicated vent shaft and whether you may connect to it (many shared shafts are for specific rooms only).
2. Decide the fan location: ceiling or high on the wall, **away from the door** (to pull air across the room), and above the shower in zone 1/2 only if the fan has the proper IP rating.
3. Penetration through the wall: core-drill with a slight outward fall (≈ 1–2°) so condensate runs out, seal with foam and sealant, fit the hood.
4. Duct: connect with tape, support with clips, **slope away from the fan towards the outside**, insulate where needed.
5. Fan: electrical connection per the electrical rough-in. Mount and test airflow with a tissue paper (it should hold against the grille).
6. Leave the finished ceiling closed with a service hatch if the fan is above a suspended ceiling.

## Tips & tricks
- A **humidity-sensor fan** means no one has to remember to switch it on.
- Choose a fan with low noise (< 30–35 dB) if it's close to a bedroom.
- If the bathroom door leaves a gap, you don't need a grille. A door with an undercut works.

## Common mistakes
- Duct into the attic or a cavity: invisible mould and rot.
- Flexible ribbed duct with sagging runs: noisy, poor flow, collects water.
- No make-up air.
- Fan switched with the light only. A timer is needed to keep it running after the shower.

## Quality checks
- Tissue test at the grille, no whistling, no back-draught in the off state.
- Humidity drops within ≈ 20 minutes after showering.

## Cross-trade
- Electrical: cable and timer planned in the rough-in.
- Ceiling finish (painting): fan hole location must be fixed before closing the ceiling. Moisture-resistant paint.
- Door installation: undercut or grille.
