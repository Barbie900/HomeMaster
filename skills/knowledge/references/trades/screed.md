# Trade: Screed and floor levelling

Graph tasks: `screed` (and the substrate for `waterproofing`, `floor-tiling`). Typical values: the screed product's data sheet and your standard (EN 13813, EN 13318) win.

**DIY level:** realistic for a small bathroom with **bagged ready-mix screed** (semi-dry/earth-moist mix) in one day with a helper. For > 15 m² or anhydrite/flowing screed, hire a contractor with a pump. **The CM measurement can be done by a contractor** if you don't want to rent the device.

## Choose the system
| Option | Good for | Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Cement screed (CT-C25-F4)** | bathrooms, wet rooms, UFH | slow to dry (weeks). The default choice for wet rooms |
| Calcium-sulfate (anhydrite) screed | large dry rooms, UFH | **moisture sensitive**. Needs a dedicated sealing before tiles in wet areas, and the surface must be sanded and primed. For bathrooms prefer cement |
| Rapid-set (fast-drying) cement screed | tight schedules | more expensive. Data sheet gives the tiling time (often 1–3 days). Follow the maker's rules exactly |
| Self-levelling compound over an existing sound slab | levelling, 3–10 mm | doesn't create falls to a drain. Needs the matching primer and mixing discipline |
| Dry screed boards | timber floors, height-limited | no falls to drains. Moisture-resistant boards only |

**Recommendation for a tiled walk-in shower on a slab with UFH:** cement or **rapid-set cement screed** with a built-in fall. If the schedule matters (you live there), rapid-set pays for itself: it replaces 4–8 weeks of drying with days.

## Build-up and dimensions
Typical floating screed build-up (bottom to top): slab → damp/PE membrane (when required) → **insulation** (EPS 30–50 mm, compressive class per load, with impact-sound option) → **PE separation sheet** → **UFH pipes** → **screed** → waterproofing → adhesive → tile.
- **Minimum screed cover over the heating pipes: typically 45 mm** for CT-C25-F4. Thinner needs a special screed. Follow the data sheet.
- Without heating: typically ≥ 45–50 mm on insulation, ≥ 25–30 mm bonded on a slab.
- **Edge strip** (≥ 8–10 mm, full height of the screed + insulation) on all walls, doorframes, pipes: this is the movement joint. Trim after screeding.
- **Fall in the shower area:** 1.5–2%, single plane to a linear drain, or ≥ 1.5% to a point drain from all sides. Built into the screed (**fall screed**), at least ≈ 2 cm at the thinnest point. The fall surface must be flat (no waves), because the waterproofing and tiles follow it.

## Quantity (see quantities.md for the method)
- Dry bagged screed ≈ **2.0–2.2 kg per m² per mm** (e.g. 6 m² × 55 mm × 2.1 kg ≈ 700 kg ≈ 28 bags of 25 kg). Add 5–10%.
- Edge strip = perimeter + doorways. PE sheet = area + 20 cm up the walls.

## Tools
- Laser level or a hose level, mark on every wall (FFL line minus the floor covering thickness)
- Screed rails or **screed guides/pads** (levelling cones) set to the finished level
- Aluminium straight edge (2.5–3 m) with a vibrating or manual screed rule
- Wooden float, steel trowel, mixing paddle drill (heavy duty) or a **forced-action mixer (rent)**
- Wheelbarrow, buckets, kneepads, boards to walk on
- Rented: **CM device** (for the moisture test) or hire the measurement; 2 m straightedge; surface moisture meter is NOT a substitute

## Method
1. **Prepare:** vacuum the slab, sweep. Mark the **finished screed height** on the walls: FFL minus the thickness of waterproofing + adhesive + tile. Check the height at the drain and at the door.
2. **Edge strip** and **separation sheet** (overlapping ≥ 8 cm, taped), pipes pressurised (UFH: keep pressure during pouring), **conduit for the thermostat sensor** in place and fixed.
3. **Set the guides:** rails or cones at the finished level, 1.2–1.5 m apart, with the fall built in around the drain (ridge lines toward the drain).
4. **Mix** the semi-dry mix as per the bag: "earth-damp", a handful squeezed holds together and doesn't drip. Too wet = shrinkage and curling.
5. **Place and compact** in sections, working from the far corner to the door. Compact with the float or a plate. **Don't walk on it.**
6. **Level** with the straightedge across the guides, fill hollows, re-level. **Remove the rails/cones** and fill the voids.
7. **Finish** with a wooden float, and a steel trowel for the final finish (not over-troweled; it must stay slightly rough for adhesion).
8. **Cut the edge strip** only after drying.
9. **Cure (the first week):** cover with PE sheet for ≈ 3 days (keep it moist, no direct sun or drafts, no heating yet). Then ventilate gently.

## Waiting times and the moisture test
- Foot traffic after ≈ 2–3 days. Don't load or tile.
- **Drying (typical, cement screed):** ≈ 1 week per cm of thickness up to 4 cm, ≈ 2 weeks per cm above. A 55 mm screed realistically needs **6–8 weeks** at 20 °C with ventilation. Heating (below) shortens it.
- **UFH heating-up protocol (functional heating):** earliest start — cement screed ≈ **21 days**, anhydrite ≈ 7 days. A common sequence (EN 1264-4 practice): start at ≈ 25 °C flow temperature for 3 days, then maximum design flow temperature for 4 days, then a controlled cool-down. Keep a **signed protocol** with date, temperatures and who did it. **Functional heating ≠ drying.** The screed is not dry after it.
- **Drying heating (to reach tiling moisture):** follow the screed and heating manufacturers: gradual steps, ventilating the room to take out the moisture (windows open, dehumidifier).
- **CM test (carbide method):** sample **from the lower third of the thickness**, at 2–3 places incl. the shower area, away from heating pipes (use your photos). Crush the sample immediately, weigh per the device, add the ampoule, shake and read pressure, then use the table. **Limits for tiles (typical, ask the data sheet): cement screed ≤ 2.0 CM-%, heated ≤ 1.8 CM-%; anhydrite ≤ 0.5 CM-%, heated ≤ 0.3 CM-%.**
- Re-measure after 1 week if it doesn't pass. No tiling, no waterproofing until it passes.

## Tips & tricks
- A screed made on a **bond-breaker/PE sheet** can move freely. Don't forget the sheet.
- Compact corners and edges extra well: they dry out and crumble first.
- **Water a little (mist)** hot or draughty rooms.
- Take photos of the screed with the guides in place and with the tape measure for the log.

## Common mistakes
- Forgetting the edge strip or cutting it before the screed has dried.
- Mix too wet: shrinkage, curling and cracking at the edges.
- Tiling after counting days instead of measuring CM.
- Skipping the heating-up protocol.
- Fall not built into the screed: the tile adhesive and the membrane can't make a fall.
- Pipes not under pressure during pouring.

## Quality checks
- Flatness ≤ 3 mm over 2 m (tight tolerance: ≤ 2 mm for large formats). Check with a straight edge.
- Fall measured with a level: 1.5–2% to the drain. Pour water on it, there should be no puddles.
- Height at the door and the drain against the reference line: ±2 mm.
- No hollow sounds (tap test), no dusting, no cracks wider than a hairline.

## Cross-trade
- Plumbing and heating: pipes tested and photographed before pouring.
- Waterproofing: needs the **dry** and **primed** screed.
- Tiling: large-format tiles need a flatter screed.
- Door: new height changes the door clearance: check before pouring.
