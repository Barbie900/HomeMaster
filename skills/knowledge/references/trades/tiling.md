# Trade: Tiling (floor, walls, grout, silicone)

Graph tasks: `wall-tiling`, `floor-tiling`, `grouting`, `silicone-joints`. Standards: EN 12004 (adhesives), EN 13888 (grout), EN 14411 (tiles). The **adhesive and grout data sheets** win over anything here.

**DIY level:** DIY with practice. A bathroom is a good first tiling project only with rectified, not too large tiles and a patient layout. Large formats (≥ 60 cm) are doable with a **levelling system**, two people for lifting, and a flat substrate.

## Classes (what to ask for in the shop)
- **Adhesive:** **C2TE** = cement-based, improved (2), reduced slip (T), extended open time (E). **S1** = deformable (2.5–5 mm).
  - Floors with underfloor heating, large formats (≥ 60 cm side), drywall/timber substrates: **C2TE S1**.
  - Walls with small/medium ceramic: C2T or C2TE.
  - Rapid-set variants (F) exist: use if the schedule needs it, but they leave less time to adjust.
- **Grout:** **CG2WA** (cementitious, improved, reduced water absorption, high abrasion resistance). **Epoxy (RG)** for showers and kitchens when you want the most stain and water resistance. Harder to apply: work in small areas, clean fast.
- **Silicone:** sanitary silicone with fungicide, in the grout colour.
- **Same-maker system:** waterproofing + adhesive + grout from one manufacturer.

## Layout (the most important hour)
1. Find the **most visible line**: the door sightline, the shower threshold, or the centre of the main wall. Start the layout there.
2. **Dry-lay a row of tiles** along both axes (with spacers) to see where the cuts fall. **Avoid slivers**: cuts < ⅓ tile at visible edges. Shift the start by half a tile or shift the grid so cuts are symmetrical and ≥ ½ tile.
3. In the shower with a **linear drain**: align the joint grid to the drain so cuts are minimal and symmetric.
4. **Walls:** the vertical axis aligned to the floor grid if possible (joint continuing joint) or deliberately offset. Mark the **horizontal level line** with a laser. Start the first row above the floor using a **batten (ledger)** so the first row isn't resting on an unstable base. The bottom row goes in last to fit the floor height.
5. **Taps, niches, sockets and boxes** should fall on tile centres or **joints**, not near edges: shift the grid by ± a few cm if the plan allows. Check box depth: boxes flush with the **finished** wall.

## Materials (quantities: see quantities.md)
Tiles (+ 10–15% waste, one batch, + 1–2 spare), adhesive, grout, silicone, **spacers**, **levelling clips and wedges** (for ≥ 60 cm tiles), **edge profiles** for outer corners, **backer rod** for deeper joints, masking tape, primer when needed, **door threshold/transition profile**.

## Method: substrate and mixing
1. **Substrate:** sound, clean, dry, flat. Floor: ≤ 3 mm over 2 m (≤ 2 mm for ≥ 60 cm). Walls: ≤ 3 mm over 2 m. The waterproofing cured. No dust: vacuum.
2. **Mixing:** add powder to water in the bucket with a paddle at low speed. Let it **slake for 3–5 min**, mix again. Only mix what you'll use in ≈ 30 min (open time as per sheet).

## Method: floor
1. **Check the layout again** against the doorway: the first row relative to the door and the threshold.
2. **Spread the adhesive:** a thin **contact layer** with the flat edge, then comb with the notched side **in one direction**, parallel to the short tile side. Spread only 1 m² at a time.
3. **Back-butter** the tile (thin flat coat) for **≥ 90% contact** in wet areas and for large formats.
4. **Place** the tile with a slight sliding motion **perpendicular to the ridges**, press and tap with the mallet. Insert levelling clips. Check with a straightedge across tiles.
5. **Lift one tile now and then** to see the adhesive transfer (> 90%, no bare patches). Adjust notch size or pressure if it's low.
6. **Edge gaps:** 5–8 mm at the walls (for the silicone). **Don't grout the wall–floor junction.**
7. **Movement joints:** over any joint in the screed and at doorways (see screed and floor plan).
8. Cut around the drain with the saw: dry-fit and mark cuts, don't force it.
9. Remove clips/wedges after the adhesive has set (typically 24 h) by tapping the foot with the mallet.

## Method: walls
1. Start from the **batten line** (row 2) and work up in rows. Tile the wall corner first. Use an edge profile on outer corners.
2. Use a notch 6–8 mm for ceramic 30 × 60, 10 mm for ≥ 60 cm, **back-buttered** in wet zones.
3. Check vertical and horizontal every 3–4 rows. Fix problems immediately, adhesive sets.
4. Window reveals: tile the reveal first, then the face for a clean edge.
5. Remove the batten after the adhesive has set and tile the bottom row to fit.

## Method: cutting
- **Straight cuts:** manual cutter for ceramic. Wet saw for porcelain and long cuts.
- **L-cuts and holes:** wet saw for the L-cut (cut to the corner, finish with grinder), **diamond hole saw** for round holes (cut from the face, with water).
- Rub the cut edge smooth with a diamond stone. Wear a mask: **FFP3 + wet cutting** is safest.

## Method: grouting
1. Wait ≥ 24 h (adhesive). Remove spacers, **clean joints** to ≥ ⅔ of the depth.
2. Mix grout per sheet (consistency like thick yoghurt) and let it slake.
3. **Wet the tiles lightly**, then press the grout in diagonally with the rubber float. Remove excess at 45°.
4. **Wait 10–20 min** (the grout skins), wipe with a damp sponge in circular movements, rinse often. Do a final cleaning of the haze with a clean damp cloth or sponge in a day.
5. **Epoxy grout:** smaller areas (≈ 1–2 m² at a time), clean immediately with the special cleaning solution. Don't leave a film.
6. **Damp-cure** cement grout for ≈ 24 h.

## Method: silicone
1. After the grout has dried (≥ 24 h) and the sanitary items are fitted.
2. **Clean joints** of dust and soap, wipe with isopropyl alcohol. Mask both sides, leave a 5–6 mm joint.
3. Insert backer rod in deep joints (> 8 mm). Joint depth ≈ half the width.
4. Cut the nozzle at 45° to the joint width. Apply in one smooth pass.
5. **Smooth with a tool or a soapy-water-wetted finger**, remove the tape immediately.
6. Don't let it be wet for 24–48 h (per the cartridge).

## Waiting times (typical)
- Floor: 24 h before walking (rapid-set less) and before grouting. Grout cure 24–72 h before heavy cleaning. Silicone cure 24–48 h.
- Heating on only after adhesive and grout have cured (≥ 7 days): then gradually.

## Tips & tricks
- **Order:** the default in this plan is walls (except the bottom row) → floor → bottom row. Floor-first also works: then protect the finished floor with boards and keep the bottom wall row 5–8 mm above it for the silicone. Choose before you start and keep to it.
- **Kneeling boards** distribute weight when you work on fresh floor tiles.
- Work from the far corner toward the door so you don't have to step on the tiles.
- Mix the tile batches from several boxes so shade and calibre vary evenly.
- Paint the ceiling before the wall tiling, so drips don't land on finished tiles.

## Common mistakes
- Tiling before the CM test and the waterproofing are done.
- Using the wrong adhesive class (C2T for UFH or large formats).
- Not back-buttering: hollow spots, cracked tiles.
- Grouting the wall–floor corner. A grouted corner cracks within a year.
- Slivers at the door because the layout wasn't dry-fitted.
- Heating the floor too soon.

## Quality checks
- Lippage (height difference between adjacent tiles): ≤ 1 mm for rectified porcelain.
- Flatness: ≤ 2–3 mm over 2 m.
- Joint width uniform ±0.5 mm; straight with a string line.
- Tap test: no hollow sound. The fall to the drain: pour water, no puddles.
- No adhesive left in the joints, silicone continuous with no voids.

## Cross-trade
- Waterproofing: use the maker-approved adhesive. Boxes sealed.
- Screed: CM, flatness, falls.
- Plumbing and electrical: outlets and boxes placed in relation to the tile grid.
- Door: threshold and clearance.
- Sanitary: drilling plan from photos, without hammer action.
