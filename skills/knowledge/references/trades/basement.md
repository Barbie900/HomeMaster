# Trade: Basement moisture (diagnosis, remedies, finishing, radon)

Graph tasks: `basement-diagnosis`, `basement-waterproofing`, `site-drainage` (with `outdoor-surfaces.md`). Typical values: the **system makers' data sheets** and a building-physics or structural opinion for serious cases win.

**DIY level:** diagnosis, ventilation, interior cementitious coatings, floor finishes and small drainage are DIY. **Excavated exterior waterproofing, perimeter drains, structural cracks that move, and conversion to living space** need professionals and usually permits.

## Step 1: find the cause (do not skip)
| Symptom | Likely cause | Quick test |
|---|---|---|
| Damp patches on the cold walls in summer, wall surface wet and condensation, musty | **Condensation** (warm humid air meets cold wall) | Tape a ≈ 40 × 40 cm plastic or foil patch to the wall for 24–48 h. **Moisture on the room side of the foil = condensation.** |
| Moisture rising from the floor, tide-line 30–150 cm up, salts | **Rising damp** (capillary) or a missing damp-proof course | Foil test: **moisture on the wall side of the foil**. Moisture meter profile along the height |
| Wet after heavy rain, water trickles in, puddles at the base | **Lateral or hydrostatic water**, poor drainage, ground sloping to the house | Look at the gutters and downpipes during rain and the ground levels outside. Cracks and the joint between wall and floor |
| Local wet spots, persistent | **Leaking pipe, drain or gutter** | Water meter test (all taps off): does it still run? Look for hot or cold spots |
| White crystalline crusts (efflorescence) | salts carried by moisture. A symptom, not the cause | Sample, cause as above |
- **Monitor** with a hygrometer (RH and temperature), over a wet and a dry period. Mark crack ends with a pencil and date. Use a crack gauge for cracks that look new.

## Step 2: fix outside causes first (graph: `site-drainage`)
- **Gutters and downpipes** working and discharging ≥ 1.5–3 m from the wall or into drains.
- **Ground sloping away** from the wall (≥ 2%, ideally 5% for the first 2 m). No flower beds that trap water at the wall.
- **Splash-back and paving** not above the damp-proof course.
- **Drains/pipes** tested for leaks.
- **Perimeter drain and exterior waterproofing** (the real fix for hydrostatic water): excavation, cleaning, bitumen or polymer coating, dimple drainage sheet, filter fleece, gravel and a pipe. A professional job in most cases.

## Step 3: choose the interior method (matched to the cause)
| Cause | Remedy | DIY | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Condensation | **Controlled ventilation**, warm surfaces (insulate), dehumidifier, don't ventilate when outside air is more humid | ★★★ | See the ventilation rule below |
| Rising damp | Renovation plaster (salt- and moisture-regulating), or injected damp-proof course by a specialist | ★★ | Renovation plaster needs the maker's full system and does **not** stop the water |
| Lateral / hydrostatic water (small) | **Interior cementitious/crystalline waterproofing slurry** (negative side, rated for it), possible interior drain and sump pump | ★★ | Works against moderate seepage only. Not against high pressure |
| Lateral water (serious) | Exterior waterproofing + drain, structural measures | ★ | Professional, permit |
| Leak | Repair the pipe or gutter | ★–★★★ | Always first |
| Cracks that move | Structural cause: foundation, settlement. Engineer | — | Don't seal first |

- **Interior waterproofing slurry:** prepare the substrate (remove paint and salts, wet to saturation, open cracks and fill with quick plug mortar, form a **cove** at the wall–floor joint), apply 2 coats with a brush as the maker says, cure moist for the specified days. Include the **floor** and **wall–floor joint**.
- **Don't** just paint with "damp-proof" paint, interior insulate, or board over a wall that is taking water.

## Ventilation rule for basements
- In summer, **warm outside air carries more water** than the cool basement walls can hold: ventilating at midday makes the basement wetter. **Ventilate at night or early morning**, when outside air is cooler and drier. Use a **hygrometer in the basement and one outside** and open only when the outside **absolute humidity** is lower.
- In winter, short intensive ventilation is safe.
- Dehumidifier: effective for condensation, with a drain hose.

## Radon
- A radioactive soil gas that accumulates in basements and ground-floor rooms in many regions. Measure with a **long-term detector (typically 3 months, a heating-season measurement is best)**, see local reference levels.
- Typical remedies: sealing the slab and cracks, **sub-slab depressurisation** (specialist), and ventilation. Re-measure after the works.

## Finishing a basement as living space
- **Permit and rules:** ceiling height (often ≥ 2.2–2.4 m), daylight (window area), fresh air, fire escape, damp and thermal standards. Without a permit it's not a "room" in the insurance or sales sense.
- Floors: **damp-proof membrane**, insulation, screed or a floating floor on a damp-proof layer. Avoid solid wood. Tiles, vinyl or a decoupled system are more robust.
- Walls: prefer **mineral** finishes (lime plaster, cement) and **no organic materials** touching damp masonry.
- Heating and ventilation (mechanical with heat recovery is best).

## Tools and PPE
Hygrometer + moisture meter, crack gauge, wire brush and scraper, a hammer-drill/chisel, brush for slurry, mixing paddle, buckets, FFP2 mask (mould and dust), gloves and eye protection, a dehumidifier, a sump pump if needed, a flashlight. **Mould:** keep large areas and health risks in view (asthma, immune problems) and use FFP2/FFP3.

## Common mistakes
- Painting over damp and salt-laden walls.
- Ventilating on hot, humid days.
- Interior insulation or boards on a wet wall.
- Ignoring gutters and the grading.
- Closing walls before the moisture has been monitored through a wet period.
- Plugging a crack that is still moving.

## Quality checks
- Wall moisture and RH are stable, and the foil test is dry.
- No salt crusts after a season. No new cracks or measurable movement.
- Radon below the reference level on re-measurement.

## Cross-trade
- Outside: gutters, grading, terraces and stairs (`outdoor-surfaces.md`). Electrical: RCD and IP ratings in damp areas, sockets above flood level. Insulation, plaster, painting and floors: only after the diagnosis is closed.

## Element: interior waterproofing slurry (step by step)
**Only** for moisture the diagnosis showed can be handled from the inside (small seepage, damp). **Not** for strong water pressure.
1. **Prepare the wall:** remove paint, plaster and salts back to sound masonry, **open and rake out joints** to ≈ 2 cm, **fill holes and cracks** with a quick-setting plug mortar, **form a cove** (≈ 4–5 cm radius) at the wall–floor joint.
2. **Wet** the wall until saturated but without standing water, **a damp surface** when the slurry goes on.
3. **Mix** the cementitious or crystalline slurry exactly per the sheet. **Apply the first coat** with a stiff brush in all directions, **work it into the pores**, include the **floor and the cove**.
4. **Second coat** after the **time in the sheet** (typically 4–24 h) while the first is still damp but firm. **Total thickness and consumption per the sheet** (typically ≈ 3–4 kg/m²).
5. **Cure moist** (mist and cover for ≈ 2–3 days, as the maker says), no draughts, no heating.
6. **Check** after a week and after the next rain or the wet season. Don't plaster or paint until the walls stay dry (a foil test).

## Element: floor in the basement
1. **Check the slab** for **cracks, damp and level**. **Test** by taping a plastic sheet for 48 h (**moisture beneath = rising damp**).
2. **Damp slab:** a **bitumen or epoxy damp-proof layer** or a **cementitious waterproofing**, then a **screed or a decoupling layer** (not wood or carpet).
3. **Falls to a floor drain** (≥ 1–2%) in utility rooms and under washing machines, **a non-return valve** on the drain against back-flow.
4. **Finish:** **tiles on a decoupling mat** or **vinyl** on a smooth, dry substrate, **mineral-based levelling**, **no solid wood**.
5. **Insulation** under the floor only when the room becomes living space, and with a **vapour-controlled** design.

## Element: sump and interior drain
1. **Use** when water collects after rain or at high groundwater. **A pump pit** (≈ 50 cm diameter, ≥ 50 cm deep), **a perforated drain** along the wall in gravel under the floor leading to it.
2. **Pump:** a **submersible drain pump** with a **float switch**, a **non-return valve** on the **discharge pipe**, discharge to a **permitted outlet** (not the sewer unless the rules say so).
3. **Power:** a **dedicated, RCD-protected circuit** and **battery backup** for a power cut during a storm. **Alarm** for pump failure.
4. **Test** with a bucket of water every few months, **clean the pit** yearly.

## Element: ventilation and dehumidifier
1. **Hygrometers** (inside and outside). **Ventilate only when the outside absolute humidity is lower** (cool mornings or nights in summer).
2. **Dehumidifier:** a condensing unit for **45–60% RH**, a **drain hose** to a drain, **emptied or continuous**. **Size** to the volume and the moisture load.
3. **Mechanical ventilation** for living use: a **small heat-recovery unit** with ducts.
4. **Don't** dry laundry in the basement without a dehumidifier.

## Element: walls and salts
1. **Remove salt efflorescence** with a **dry brush** (no water, no acid), **vacuum**.
2. **Renovation plaster** (breathable, salt-storing) for **rising damp** only with the **full system of one maker**, in the **thickness the sheet requires** (typically ≥ 2 cm).
3. **Breathable mineral paint** (silicate or lime), **no dispersion paint** on damp walls.
4. **No interior insulation or boards** on walls that take in water.
