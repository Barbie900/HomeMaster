# Trade: Sanitary ware (WC, basin, shower, shower glass, bath, taps)

Graph tasks: `plumbing-rough-in`, `sanitary-fit-out`, `shower-glass-install`. Each section below is **one element** with its own order, tools and checks, so it can be given on its own. Typical values: the **product's installation sheet always wins**.

**DIY level:** fully DIY for a careful person (a helper for the frame, glass and bath). **Water supply and drains** follow the usual plumbing limits (`plumbing.md`). **Electric items** (a shower-WC, a towel rail, lit mirrors) are connected by a licensed electrician.

## WC frame (wall-hung WC): rough-in
**When:** after the drain lines and the finished floor level (FFL) line are set, before the screed and before any wall is closed.
1. **Choose the frame by the wall:** for a **solid wall** a free-standing frame fixed to the floor and the wall. For **drywall** a frame with floor feet and the board's own supports (**never hang a frame on plain plasterboard**). For a **pre-wall** (a shallow wall in front of the old one) a low-depth frame. **Frame height** (typically 82, 98, 112 or 120 cm) fits the wall height and the cistern position.
2. **Set the height from the reference line:** bowl rim **≈ 40–43 cm above the FFL** (standard), **≈ 46–48 cm** for comfort or accessible use. Add the **finished floor** (tile and adhesive) to the frame's adjustable feet so the rim ends up at the target.
3. **Position:** the WC centre line on the plan, **≥ 20 cm to the side wall** (≥ 40 cm clear on both sides is comfortable), and the bowl projection (≈ 50–55 cm) clear of the door swing and the shower.
4. **Fix** the frame level in both directions with **M10 anchors** (floor) and wall anchors or brackets. **Check the frame's rated load** (typically ≥ 400 kg).
5. **Drain:** connect the **Ø90 or Ø110 outlet** to the drain with the maker's connectors and a **fall of ≥ 1–2%** in the horizontal part. **Sound-insulating sleeve** around the pipe and **rubber or PE pads** between the frame and the wall.
6. **Water:** the cistern's **½" supply** with an **isolation valve that stays accessible** (through the plate opening). Insulate the cold pipe against condensation.
7. **Test:** fill the cistern, flush several times, check the **joints dry** with paper, then **pressure-test** the supply (see `plumbing.md#Element: tests`).
8. **Close:** **moisture-resistant board** (impregnated or cement board, 2 layers for tiles) with the **plate opening** cut to the template. **Mark the position of every fixing** and photograph the frame before the cladding.

## WC bowl and flush plate: fit-out
**When:** after the wall and floor tiling, grouting and (if used) the silicone plan. **Not before**, or the tiles get damaged.
1. **Stud bolts:** cut to length (leave ≈ 2–3 threads beyond the nut), **sleeve and seal** them as the frame says. The **bowl outlet connector** with its rubber seal, pushed fully into the frame outlet.
2. **Pads and seals:** the bowl's **sound-insulating pads** and the **seal around the outlet**. **Dry-fit** first.
3. **Mount the bowl:** hang it on the studs, **hand-tighten the nuts evenly** with the plastic washers, **a quarter turn more**. **Never over-tighten** (ceramic cracks). **Check the bowl is level** and does not rock.
4. **Seat and lid** (soft-close): fit as the sheet says.
5. **Flush plate:** fit into the frame, **adjust the flush volume** (typically 3 / 6 l dual flush) and **water level** in the cistern as the sheet says.
6. **Seal** the bowl to the wall with a **thin bead of sanitary silicone** (leave a 5 cm gap at the bottom for a leak to show, if you want it).
7. **Test:** flush ten times, check for leaks and running water.

## Shower drain (point or linear): rough-in
**When:** with the drain lines, **before the screed**, so the floor falls are built to it.
1. **Choose:** **point** (a square drain in the centre or the corner) or **linear** (a channel along a wall, tiles fall to it in one plane, easier with large tiles). **Flow ≥ 0.4–0.8 l/s** (typical showers use ≈ 0.15–0.25 l/s). **Trap seal ≥ 50 mm** (or the approved low-height version).
2. **Build height:** from the slab to the top of the flange is typically **65–120 mm** depending on the product. **It must fit under the screed, falls, waterproofing, adhesive and tile**. **Check this first**, and the pipe fall to the stack.
3. **Set the body** on the slab at the planned position: **the top of the drain flange = the top of the screed at the drain minus the waterproofing and tile build-up** (per the product's sheet, usually flush with the finished screed at the drain). **Level in both directions.** Fix and **seal** the body.
4. **Connect** the outlet (DN50 typically) to the drain with **≥ 1–2% fall**. **Cap the drain** so no screed or dirt falls in.
5. **Waterproofing flange:** the **collar of the same system** as the membrane, lapping onto the drain flange. Plan it now.
6. **Falls in the screed:** **1.5–2%**, a single plane to a linear drain, **all sides to a point drain** (screed pour, `screed.md`).
7. **Test:** pour water, check it flows and the joints don't leak, before the screed.
**Tips:** put the linear drain **at the wall opposite the door and the taps**, so the water runs away from where you step. A **wall-side channel** with a single fall is easier to tile than a square drain in the middle.

## Basin and vanity unit
**When:** after the wall and floor tiling and grouting.
1. **Supply and waste** positions come from the rough-in: **waste Ø32–40 mm**, **supply isolation valves (angle valves)** ½" or ⅜", **hot on the left**. **Mark the heights** from the FFL line.
2. **Basin height:** top rim **≈ 80–85 cm** (up to 90 cm for tall users), the tap centre **≈ 15–20 cm above the rim** for a wall-mounted spout. **Clear space ≥ 60 cm** in front of the basin.
3. **Backing:** a **wall-hung basin** or heavy vanity needs **backing** (steel plate, plywood ≥ 18 mm or a frame) **fixed in the wall before closing**. **Rated load ≥ 80–120 kg** with a safety margin. **Never fix into plain plasterboard.**
4. **Fix** the basin on the **brackets or the unit on the wall plates** (anchors suited to the wall: solid, hollow, drywall with backing). **Level** with a spirit level.
5. **Taps and waste:** fit the tap, supply hoses (flexible, rated) and the **pop-up waste**, then the **trap** with the right gaskets. **Hand-tight plus a quarter turn** on plastic.
6. **Connect** the supply (isolation valves open slowly) and **test** for leaks with dry paper, **fill and drain the basin** to test the overflow.
7. **Seal** the basin to the wall with a thin bead of sanitary silicone.

## Taps, mixers and shower fixtures
**When:** after tiling and grouting. The **concealed body** is installed during the rough-in, at the depth the maker specifies (wall + adhesive + tile).
1. **Concealed shower mixer:** the body at **≈ 100–110 cm** (mixer) and the outlets to the head. **Protect the body with the maker's cap** during tiling. **The finishing set** (cartridge, trim) goes on after the tiles.
2. **Shower head and rail:** **fixed head ≈ 200–220 cm** above the floor (**ceiling distance ≥ 15–20 cm**), **hand shower rail ≈ 80–120 cm** (hose length ≥ 1.5 m), the **wall elbow** firmly fixed to **backing**.
3. **Thermostatic mixer:** set the **anti-scald limit** (≈ 38 °C comfort, **≤ 43 °C** maximum), check that **hot and cold are the right way round**. The **supply pressure** typically **≥ 1.5 bar** (rain showers prefer ≥ 2 bar), dynamic.
4. **Flush the pipes** (remove aerators and cartridges first) before fitting the trim, so grit doesn't ruin the cartridge.
5. **Seal** the trims with silicone only on the **top edge** (leave the bottom open so leaks show).
6. **Test:** all temperatures, the hand shower diverter and the **no-leak check behind the plate** (touch with dry paper after 10 minutes).

## Shower glass (screen)
**When:** **after tiling and grouting**. **Measure only on the finished tiles**, then order (lead time typically 1–3 weeks). **Glass is made to measure and cannot be returned.**
1. **Choose:** **fixed panel** (walk-in, ≥ 90–120 cm wide), **hinged or pivot door**, **sliding door**. **Tempered safety glass (EN 12150) 8 mm** (or 10 mm for larger frameless panels), or laminated. Weight ≈ **20 kg per m² for 8 mm**, so a 2 × 1 m panel weighs ≈ 40 kg.
2. **Plan the fixings before tiling:** **backing** or **sealed anchors** in the wall where the glass profile, clamps or support bar will go. **The membrane must not be punctured** without sealing. Use the **maker's sealed fixings or a profile glued and sealed to the tiles**. **Mark and photograph** the positions.
3. **Measure** the width and height at **several heights**. Walls are rarely plumb: **adjustable profiles** absorb **≈ 10–20 mm**. **Record the drain, the threshold and the falls** (the glass bottom must clear the tiles by ≈ 5–10 mm for the seal).
4. **Install with two or three people**, **suction lifters and gloves**, a **soft base** under the glass. **Never let glass touch the tile or a metal edge.** Use the **plastic or rubber gaskets** supplied. **Do not overtighten** clamps (glass breaks at the clamp).
5. **Plumb and square:** check with a level, **adjust the hinges and the wall profile**, fit the **seals and the drip strip** (the door swings **outward** or both ways in most rules, and **it must open without hitting the fixtures**).
6. **Silicone:** a **neutral-cure sanitary silicone** (acetic types can attack laminated glass interlayers and some metals). Seal the **wall profile to the tile** and the **tile to the glass** where the maker says. **Don't seal the bottom seal strip where it needs to drain.**
7. **Test:** run the shower at full flow for 5 minutes, **check outside the screen** for splashes and drips, and **open and close** the door ten times. **Re-tighten after a week.**
**Safety:** tempered glass **breaks into small pieces** and a **corner hit can shatter a panel**. Store flat or on edge on foam, away from traffic, and **don't sit or lean** on a frameless panel.

## Bath
**When:** **before the wall tiling** and **after the rough-in** (plumbing and drainage tested, the screed dry). **The waterproofing membrane laps onto the bath's edge**.
1. **Set the bath** on adjustable feet or a support frame, **level in both directions**, rim **≈ 55–60 cm** above the FFL. **Acrylic baths weigh ≈ 25–35 kg empty and ≈ 250–300 kg full**. **Check the floor load** on timber floors.
2. **Waste and overflow:** the **waste kit**, a **trap with access**, a **fall ≥ 1–2%** to the drain. **Fill the bath and test** before closing.
3. **Edge:** the **tile lap** (≈ 3–5 mm) over the rim with a **flexible silicone joint**. **Seal the gap** between the rim and the wall with the system's **sealing tape** (membrane) before tiling.
4. **Bath panel** with a **removable access panel** to the trap. **Never close the trap in.**
5. **Filled with water** when the **silicone** is applied (the bath's weight settles it), so the joint doesn't tear.

## Common mistakes across sanitary ware
- Hanging basins, frames or fixtures on **plain plasterboard** or without backing.
- **Not testing** before closing, and **photographs** missing.
- **Over-tightened ceramics and plastic**.
- **Glass ordered from drawings** and not from the finished tiles.
- **No isolation valves** or no access to a trap.
- **A wrongly heated or unprotected** thermostatic mixer (scalds).

## Quality checks
- Items level and firm, no rocking, silicone continuous, **no leaks after 24 hours** (dry paper test), **all isolation valves work and are accessible**, the glass door **opens, closes and drains** correctly.
