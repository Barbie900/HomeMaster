# Trade: Garden structures (shed, pergola, carport, fence, retaining wall)

Graph tasks: `garden-structure-plan`, `garden-foundation`, `garden-structure-build`. For terraces and paving see `outdoor-surfaces.md`. Typical values: the **local snow and wind loads, permit and boundary rules** and an **engineer for retaining walls and large structures** win.

**DIY level:** sheds, pergolas, fences, raised beds and small carports are good DIY projects. **Get an engineer** for retaining walls (typically over ≈ 0.5–1 m), anything people can fall from, large spans, and heavy roofs in snow regions. **Electrics outdoors: licensed electrician.**

## Planning first (task: `garden-structure-plan`)
- **Permit or notification:** depends on **size (floor area), height, roof, use (living vs. storage), and distance to the boundary**. A carport, a large shed or a pergola with a roof often counts as a building. Fences and walls have height limits at the boundary. **Ask the municipality in writing** and get neighbour consent where required.
- **Location:** utilities (call before you dig, never build over a pipe, cable, septic tank or soakaway), tree roots (distance ≥ tree height to avoid root damage and falling branches), slopes (drainage, retaining), sun and wind, access for materials, and **fire distances** if there is a stove, fire pit or grill (typically ≥ 3–5 m from combustible structures, per local rules).
- **Loads:** **snow and wind loads** are regional. Roofs need the right slope and fixings for them. Don't copy a design from a different climate.
- **Materials:** timber with the right **use class (EN 335)**: ground contact or constantly wet needs the highest class (e.g. class 4) or a barrier. Above ground and sheltered: lower classes. Hardwood, thermally modified, or pressure-treated softwood for posts, stainless or hot-dip galvanised fixings. **Keep timber off the ground.**
- **Drainage:** keep water away from the building and the boundary, with gutters or a drip edge, not onto the neighbour's land.

## Foundations (task: `garden-foundation`)
| Structure | Foundation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Light shed (≲ 6–10 m²) | paving slabs on a compacted gravel bed, or timber or concrete skids on a gravel bed | levelled, with falls away, an air gap under the floor |
| Shed with a concrete floor | **slab** ≈ 10–15 cm reinforced concrete on 15–20 cm compacted gravel and a PE layer | cure ≥ 7 days before loading |
| Pergola, carport, post structures | **point footings (concrete) below the frost line**, typically ≈ 80–120 cm deep (region), 30 × 30 cm minimum, with **post shoes (metal bases) 5–10 cm above the ground** or on an anchor | or **screw piles** (rent or hire an installer) |
| Fence posts | concrete footing ≈ 60–100 cm deep (taller fences and windy sites deeper) | posts on metal shoes above the concrete last longer than posts set into it |
| Retaining wall | strip footing, drainage and geotextile behind, **engineer above ≈ 0.5–1 m** | water pressure and surcharge loads are what bring them down |
- **Check level, square and diagonals** (equal diagonals = square) before building.

## Build: timber structures (task: `garden-structure-build`)
1. **Mark and set the posts** plumb in both directions (two levels or a laser), braced until the concrete has cured.
2. **Beams and rafters:** the sizes and spans come from the design (typical posts 9 × 9 to 14 × 14 cm, beams and rafters sized by span and snow load). **Notched and bolted joints** (not only nails), **metal connectors rated for the load**, and **bracing** (diagonals or sheathing) so it doesn't rack.
3. **Roof:** a pitch of **≥ 5–10°** for sheet or felt roofs (more for tiles), correct overlaps, **edges sealed and ventilated**, and a drip edge or gutters. **Wind uplift:** hold-down straps to the posts and the posts to the foundation.
4. **Walls and cladding:** weather-resistant boards with a **ventilation gap** (a 20 mm batten behind the cladding), bottom edge ≥ 15 cm above the ground, **end grain sealed**.
5. **Doors and windows:** weather seals, **drain slots**, locking where needed. Floor: raised, with a damp-proof layer and ventilation.
6. **Finish:** oil, stain or let it weather. **Maintenance** every 2–5 years.
7. **Electrics:** a **licensed electrician** with outdoor-rated cable (buried at the depth the rules say, in conduit, with RCD).

## Fences
- **Posts every 1.5–2.5 m** (the panel size), **tops capped**, and gates hung on **stable posts** with extra concrete. Panels or boards fixed to **rails** with a gap at the bottom (≈ 5–10 cm) so they don't rot.
- **Height limit** at the boundary (typically ≈ 1.5–2 m, local), and a rule on who owns, builds and maintains it. The **good side** usually faces the neighbour (local custom).

## Retaining walls and slopes
- **Dry-stacked or block walls ≲ 0.5 m** can be DIY with a compacted base, back-slope (batter), drainage gravel and a perforated pipe behind, and filter fabric.
- **Above ≈ 0.5–1 m or with a building, road or slope above:** an **engineer's design** (footing, reinforcement, drainage, surcharge). Water and soil weight cause failures.
- **Permit** for higher walls and walls on the boundary.

## Tools
Spade, post-hole digger or auger (rent), concrete mixer (rent), levels and a laser, string line and pegs, plate compactor (for gravel), circular saw, mitre saw, jigsaw, drill/driver, impact driver, clamps, chisel, spirit levels, ladder, safety gear (gloves, glasses, ear protection, boots).

## Common mistakes
- No permit check, or building on or close to the boundary.
- Posts in the ground without a base or a bracing.
- Footings above the frost line: movement and heave.
- No bracing: a wobbling structure.
- Roof designed without the local snow load.
- Digging without locating utilities.
- Retaining walls without drainage.

## Quality checks
- Plumb, level and square (equal diagonals). Posts firm with no play.
- Water drains away and doesn't pond. Timber off the ground and not trapped in a wet corner.
- Roof fixings and connectors in place and tightened.

## Element: shed
1. **Base:** compacted gravel (15–20 cm) with **paving slabs or concrete skids**, or a **reinforced slab ≈ 10–15 cm**, **level within ≈ 5 mm**, **falls away**, **an air gap under the floor**.
2. **Floor frame:** **treated or durable joists** at ≈ 40 cm, **a damp-proof layer** under the floor, **floor boards or OSB**.
3. **Walls:** **frame (≈ 45 × 70 mm studs at ≈ 60 cm)** with **bracing or sheathing**, **cladding with a ventilation gap**, **bottom edge ≥ 15 cm above the ground**.
4. **Roof:** **pitch ≥ 5–10°**, **rafters sized by the snow load**, **sheet or felt roofing with the right overlaps**, **a drip edge or gutter**, **hold-down straps** against uplift.
5. **Door and windows:** **weather seals**, **drain slots**, **a lock**. **Fire distances** if there is a stove or power.

## Element: pergola and carport
1. **Point footings** below the frost line (typically ≈ 80–120 cm, **≥ 30 × 30 cm**), **post bases (metal shoes) 5–10 cm above ground**, **anchors cast in or drilled and bonded**.
2. **Posts** plumb in both directions and **braced** until the concrete has cured (≥ 7 days).
3. **Beams and rafters:** the sizes and spans from the design (**snow load!**), **bolted connections and rated connectors**, **diagonal bracing** against racking.
4. **Roof:** **polycarbonate or sheet** with the right fixings and **a fall ≥ 5°**, **gutter** to a drain.
5. **Check permit rules** for a roofed structure and the distance to the boundary.

## Element: fence posts and panels
1. **Line** with string, **post spacing** = panel width + the post thickness (typically 1.5–2.5 m).
2. **Footing:** **≈ 60–100 cm deep** (taller or windy sites deeper), **concrete with a gravel base for drainage**, **post bases above the concrete** if you use metal shoes.
3. **Posts** plumb, **braced** until cured. **Gate posts** with **bigger footings**.
4. **Rails and panels or boards:** **gap at the bottom ≈ 5–10 cm** (not touching the soil), **end grain sealed**, **fixings stainless**.
5. **Height** within the boundary limit. **Good side** toward the neighbour (local custom).

## Element: gate
1. **Posts** set in **larger footings**, **braced**, **plumb**, **the gap** to match the gate with the hinges.
2. **Gate frame** with a **diagonal brace** (the brace runs **up from the hinge** to the latch side), **heavy-duty hinges**, **a latch that works from both sides**.
3. **Adjust** the **gap (≈ 5 mm)** and the **drop** (a **small wheel or a stop** for wide gates).
4. **Motorised gates:** a **licensed electrician**, a **safety edge and photocells** (crush hazard).

## Element: retaining wall
1. **Heights ≲ 0.5 m** may be DIY. **Above ≈ 0.5–1 m or with a building, road or slope above:** an **engineer**.
2. **Footing** on compacted gravel (≈ 15–20 cm), **below frost** for taller walls. **First course dead level.**
3. **Back-slope (batter)** ≈ 1:10 for dry-stack or block walls, **geotextile** behind, **≥ 30 cm of drainage gravel** and a **perforated drain pipe** at the base leading to an outlet.
4. **Courses staggered**, **capstones**, **weep holes** in solid walls.
5. **Permit** for higher walls and walls on the boundary.

## Element: raised beds
1. **Position** (sun ≥ 6 h for vegetables), **level the ground**, **weed membrane or cardboard** at the base.
2. **Frame:** **untreated larch, douglas fir, or untreated pressure-free timber** (avoid old railway sleepers and treated wood for food), **height 30–60 cm**, **width ≤ 120 cm** (reach).
3. **Corner posts** and **staples or screws**, **a mesh on the base** against voles.
4. **Fill:** **layers (coarse prunings, turf, compost, topsoil)**. **Settling:** top up after a month.
