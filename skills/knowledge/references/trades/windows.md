# Trade: Windows and external doors

Graph tasks: `windows-install`. Values are typical: the **window maker's installation guide** and your country's rules win.

**DIY level:** possible for standard windows with 2–3 people and a careful approach. **Check the supplier's warranty:** many require certified fitters. Large or heavy glazing, sliding doors and façade changes are best done by professionals. Work above the ground floor needs scaffolding or a safe platform.

## Decide first
- **Position in the wall:** ideally in the insulation plane (or the outer third of the wall), so the frame is warm and thermal bridges small.
- **Type and glazing:** double or triple glazing (typical whole-window U-value Uw ≈ 0.8–1.3 W/m²K), frame (PVC, wood, wood-aluminium, aluminium with thermal break), opening style.
- **Security:** ground-floor and accessible windows with a higher resistance class (e.g. RC2 in the EU), lockable handles.
- **Shading and blinds:** exterior blinds are the best against overheating. Plan the box and the cable.
- **Ventilation concept** for the airtight windows: trickle vents, regular airing, or mechanical (decentral heat-recovery units).
- **Permit:** facades, heritage areas and apartment blocks (common parts) often need consent.
- **Lead time:** typically 4–10 weeks. **Measure on site after strip-out** (rough opening, squareness, wall thickness) and agree the order with the supplier.

## Materials and tools
- Window/door units, **support/glazing blocks and shims** (packers), frame anchors or long screws with plugs, **low-expansion PU foam** (window-specific), **airtight tape inside** (vapour-control tape), **weather-tight tape or compriband outside**, sill (window board) and external sill, sealants, silicone, plaster profiles.
- Tools: levels (1 m+) and laser, drill/driver and long bits, suction lifters (for glazing), a hand saw, a knife, foam gun, a scaffold or platform, **helpers** (a window can weigh 30–60 kg per m²).

## Method (standard replacement window)
1. **Remove the old window** (cut the glazing out or dismantle, protect the reveal), **clean the opening**, check the structure (lintel and sill).
2. **Check** the opening (±3 mm squareness). Prepare the sill and the **support blocks** under the frame (under the hinge side and the glazing bead positions, per the guide).
3. **Dry-fit** the frame: level, plumb, in the planned plane, with a **perimeter gap of ≈ 10–20 mm** (wider for the foam or for insulated joints, as the maker says).
4. **Fix** the frame: anchors within ≈ 15–20 cm from each corner and then ≈ 50–70 cm apart, through the frame sections into solid masonry (not into the foam or the plaster). **Don't over-tighten**: the frame must stay straight. Operating parts must work without sticking.
5. **Remove the sash**, check it hangs and closes with an even gap, and adjust the hinges and the striker plates.
6. **Seal in 3 layers:**
   - **Inside:** airtight vapour-control tape across the joint (stops warm humid air entering the joint).
   - **Middle:** low-expansion foam (don't over-fill, and don't use ordinary foam, which exerts pressure and bows the frame).
   - **Outside:** weather-tight tape or sealant (stops wind-driven rain, but is vapour-open).
7. **Sills:** an internal board with a slight slope and a separate joint to the wall, an external sill with a drip edge and a slope of ≈ 3–5°.
8. **Reveals:** plaster or insulate and finish (see `walls.md`, `painting.md`). Leave the movement joint at the frame.
9. **Remove protective films** within the time the maker says (UV bonds them) and clean the glass.

## Tips & tricks
- Order **one window first** and test it for a week if you're replacing many.
- Use a **laser** to align several windows in the same façade.
- Photograph the support blocks and fixings before closing the joint.
- Keep the **delivery packaging** until you've checked all the sizes.

## Common mistakes
- Ordering from the old window sizes without measuring on site.
- Frame fixed only through the foam or the wrong spots.
- No inside airtight tape: moisture in the joint and mould.
- Ordinary foam: the frame bows, the sash binds.
- No ventilation concept after the airtight windows are in: mould on cold corners.
- Blocking the drain slots in the frame.

## Quality checks
- Gaps even all round, the sash closes with consistent pressure, the frame level and plumb (±1 mm/m).
- Smoke or tissue test at the closed window for draughts.
- Condensation behaviour in the first cold weeks (watch the corners).

## Cross-trade
- Insulation (the plane and reveals), plaster and painting (reveals, sills), electrical (blind motors), ventilation (concept), permits (façade), heating (replace radiators under windows if they are oversize now).

## Element: window sills and reveals
1. **Inner sill (board):** **a slight fall (≈ 1–2%) to the room or none**, **overhang ≈ 2–4 cm**, **end caps or let-ins** at the reveals, **foam or mortar bedding** under it, **sealed joint** to the frame (acrylic or silicone), a **flexible joint** at the wall.
2. **Outer sill:** **a fall of ≈ 3–5° to the outside**, a **drip edge** that overhangs the wall by ≈ 3–4 cm, **end plugs**, a **sealing tape under the sill** against the window frame. **Never** let water run behind it.
3. **Reveals:** **plaster or board** the reveals with **corner beads and mesh** (`facade.md`, `walls.md`), the **joint to the frame** with **acrylic caulk** (no rigid plaster against the frame). **Insulated reveals** on a facade job.
4. **Check** the sills level and watertight with a water test.

## Element: blinds and external shading
1. **Choose:** **external roller blinds or screens** (best against overheating), **venetian blinds**, **internal blinds** (decor and glare only). **Colour and light factor** (g-value).
2. **Plan with the facade:** the **box position and size** (in the insulation plane or on the reveal), **guide rails**, the **cable or the motor** (230 V motors by the electrician, 24 V by wire or battery-powered models).
3. **Fix** the box and the rails to the **frame or the masonry** with the maker's fixings (not into insulation), **plumb and parallel**, a **sealed joint** so wind and water don't get in.
4. **Wiring:** **conduit** (switch, motor, wind sensor) laid in the rough-in, **a neutral and a power point** near each blind.
5. **Test** the end positions, the **manual override** and the **wind and sun sensors**. **Child safety:** no loose cords in reach.

## Element: entrance door
1. **Order from site measurements** and decide **opening direction** and **threshold type** (low and sealed). **Security class** (typically RC2 for a ground-floor door in the EU), **3-point lock**, **multi-point hinges**.
2. **Install** like a window: plumb, shimmed, fixed with long screws through the hinge and lock points, **the sill carried on supports**.
3. **Sealing:** **airtight tape inside**, **foam in the middle**, **weather tape outside**, the **threshold sealed to the floor** and **draining to the outside**.
4. **Adjust** the hinges, the strike plate and the **compression of the seals** (the door closes with a light pull, with no daylight).
5. **Test** with a hose (water test), a **tissue for draught**, and the **lock and key** several times.
