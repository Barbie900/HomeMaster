# Trade: Floor coverings (laminate, parquet, vinyl, carpet)

Graph tasks: `floor-covering-dry`, `skirting-and-trim` (with carpentry). For tiles use `tiling.md`. Typical values: the **floor maker's installation instructions** win.

**DIY level:** very good DIY. Floating click floors (laminate, engineered wood, click vinyl) are the easiest. Glued parquet and sanding solid wood take practice.

## Choose the covering (comparison)
| Covering | Pros | Cons | With UFH | Wet rooms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Click vinyl (SPC/LVT, rigid core)** | waterproof, quiet, easy, thin | can feel plastic, dents under point loads | very good (low thermal resistance) | yes (kitchen, hall; not shower) |
| **Glue-down LVT** | thin, very stable, warm | needs a perfect substrate | very good | yes |
| **Laminate** | cheap, hard-wearing | water at the joints swells it, can sound hollow | OK if approved | no (moisture-resistant versions only in kitchens, not bathrooms) |
| **Engineered parquet (floating or glued)** | real wood top layer, can be re-sanded | cost, humidity sensitive | good if approved | no |
| **Solid parquet (glued)** | long life, re-sandable many times | dimensionally unstable, needs stable humidity | possible with care, floor temperature limits | no |
| **Carpet** | warm, quiet | allergy, wear, moisture | thermal resistance high | no |
**Recommendation:** for bedrooms and living rooms on a heated floor: **engineered wood or rigid-core click vinyl**. For hall and kitchen: **click or glue-down vinyl, or tiles**. Don't use solid wood over UFH unless the maker approves it.

## Substrate (the most important thing)
- **Moisture:** cement screed within the covering's limit, typically **≤ 2.0 CM-% (≤ 1.8 heated)**, anhydrite **≤ 0.5 (≤ 0.3 heated)**. Measure it (CM), see `screed.md`. Timber floors: wood moisture within the maker's range.
- **Flatness:** floating floors typically **≤ 3 mm over 2 m**, glued floors and thin vinyl **≤ 2 mm over 2 m**. Fix with self-levelling compound or grinding.
- **Clean, sound, free of oil.** Prime as the adhesive or compound maker says.
- **Damp-proof membrane** (PE film ≥ 0.2 mm, overlap ≥ 20 cm, up the wall) on mineral substrates for floating wood or laminate, unless the underlay already has one.
- **Existing floors:** old tiles can be a base for vinyl or floating floors if they are sound and flat (fill the joints). Old timber floors need the joists and boards checked.

## Underlay and UFH
- Underlay as the maker prescribes (acoustic, levelling, with or without vapour barrier). **Never double up** underlays.
- **UFH:** the total thermal resistance of covering + underlay is limited, typically **≤ 0.10–0.15 m²K/W**. The maker's UFH approval lists the combination. Floor surface temperature typically ≤ 27–29 °C for wood.
- Heating-up protocol done and heating **turned down** (typically to ≤ 18 °C) 2–3 days before and after installation.

## Quantities
- Area + waste: straight lay **5–7%**, diagonal **10–15%**, herringbone **12–15%**. Round to full packs.
- Underlay = area. Skirting = perimeter − doors + 10%. Profiles and transition strips per door.
- Expansion filler for the wall gap.

## Tools
Tapping block, pull bar, spacers (8–15 mm), saw (jigsaw, hand saw or a circular saw), **undercut saw or oscillating tool** (doors frames), set square, tape measure, pencil, utility knife, notched trowel (glued), level, pull saw for the profile, chalk line, knee pads, an angle template.
- Rent: floor sander (solid wood), grinder for the substrate. Buy: vapour-barrier tape, silicone.

## Method: floating floor
1. **Acclimatise** the packs unopened in the room (typically ≥ 48 h) at 18–22 °C and 40–60% RH.
2. **Plan the layout:** along the main light direction or the long side. Aim for a **last row ≥ 5 cm wide** (otherwise cut the first row narrower), stagger joints by **≥ 30 cm** (≥ 20 cm for click vinyl as per the maker).
3. **Lay the membrane/underlay** with taped joints, and up the wall.
4. **Start in a corner**, spacers at all walls: **expansion gap typically 8–10 mm** for laminate/vinyl, **10–15 mm** for wood, and larger over big areas. **Expansion joints** at doorways and wherever the maker's maximum area (often ~ 8–10 m on a side) is exceeded.
5. Click boards together at the correct angle, tap with the block, **keep the rows straight** (check with a string).
6. **Cut door frames** (undercut) so the floor slides under. Leave the gap around pipes with rosettes.
7. **Finish:** remove spacers, fit **skirting to the wall (not to the floor)**, profiles and thresholds. Don't glue the floor to the profiles.

## Method: glued floors (parquet, LVT)
1. Primer and levelling, followed by a **dry-run**.
2. **Adhesive:** elastic (e.g. MS polymer or PU) for parquet, specific adhesive for vinyl. Notched trowel as the maker specifies. Work in sections so the adhesive stays within its open time.
3. Lay and press (roller for vinyl). Weight the first row. A **gap at the walls** as before.
4. **Sanding and finishing** (solid parquet): rough, medium and fine grit sanding, filling gaps, oiling or lacquering. Dust control and ventilation, **no open flames** with solvent finishes.

## Waiting times
- Floating floor: usable immediately (check maker), heating up gradually after 1–2 days.
- Glued floor: walkable after ≈ 24 h, heavy furniture after 3–7 days, UFH back on gradually after the adhesive's cure time.
- Oiled/lacquered floors: light use 24–48 h, full cure up to 2–4 weeks. Don't cover with rugs or tape in that time.

## Tips & tricks
- Mix planks from several packs for even shade.
- Lay the first two rows with a laser line when the wall isn't straight, and trim the first row to the wall.
- Protect the floor with cardboard or breathable film during later works. **Not** plastic film (traps moisture).
- Keep a few spare planks from the same batch.

## Common mistakes
- No expansion gap or the floor pinned under fixed units, skirting or heavy furniture.
- Laying on a moist screed.
- Underlay + covering not approved for UFH.
- Wetting laminate (mop and flood) or using it where it's wet.
- A thin last row: it's an optical and mechanical weak point.

## Quality checks
- Flatness of the finished floor ≤ 3 mm over 2 m, no squeaks or bounce, joints tight with no gaps wider than the maker allows.
- Gaps free of debris, transitions and thresholds fixed to the substrate, not through the floating floor.

## Cross-trade
- Screed (CM), heating (UFH protocol and temperatures), doors (frame undercuts), kitchen (floor under units or not), painting (before the floor, with touch-ups after).

## Element: substrate check and levelling
**When:** after the walls are painted (dust) and the screed has passed its moisture test. **Result:** a dry, flat, clean base.
1. **Moisture:** CM-% within the covering's limit (`screed.md`), or timber moisture in the maker's range. **No measurement, no floor.**
2. **Flatness:** a 2 m straightedge, ≤ 3 mm (floating) or ≤ 2 mm (glued, thin vinyl). Mark hollows and humps with a pencil.
3. **Humps:** grind or chisel off. **Hollows under ≈ 3–5 mm:** fill with repair mortar. **Larger areas:** self-levelling compound.
4. **Self-levelling:** vacuum, **prime** (and let it dry), **seal door gaps and edges** with foam strip or tape so the compound can't run, mix with the **exact water amount** (typically ≈ 4–5.5 l per 25 kg bag), pour from the far corner toward the door, **spread with a rake and roll with a spiked roller**, thickness typically 3–10 mm. **Don't walk** for ≈ 3–6 h, **load after ≈ 24 h**, **cover only after the maker's drying time**.
5. **Clean:** vacuum thoroughly (dust under a floating floor makes it creak).
6. **Check again** with the straightedge and note the finished floor height at every door.

## Element: moisture barrier and underlay
1. **Barrier:** on mineral substrates a **PE film ≥ 0.2 mm**, overlaps ≥ 20 cm taped, **up the walls ≈ 5 cm**. Skip it only if the underlay has its own integrated barrier (read the sheet).
2. **Underlay:** the one **approved by the floor maker** (acoustic foam, cork, fibre). **Never double up.** For UFH choose a **low thermal resistance** type and check the **total** of covering and underlay against the maker's UFH limit (typically ≤ 0.10–0.15 m²K/W).
3. **Lay** it edge to edge, **butt the seams and tape them** (overlap only if the maker says so). Roll out only as far as you work.
4. **Check:** no gaps, wrinkles or debris, and the door gaps stay clear.

## Element: laying the planks (floating floor)
1. **Acclimatise** the packs (≥ 48 h, unopened, 18–22 °C, 40–60% RH). Mix planks from several packs.
2. **Layout:** along the main light direction or the long side. **Dry-lay** the first and last rows: the **last row ≥ 5 cm** (or cut the first row narrower), **stagger joints ≥ 30 cm** (≥ 20 cm for click vinyl if the maker allows).
3. **First row:** the tongue to the wall, **spacers** (typically 8–10 mm for laminate and vinyl, 10–15 mm for wood). **Scribe** the first row to a crooked wall.
4. **Click:** the long side at a low angle, then the short side with the **tapping block**. Keep a **string line** to check the rows.
5. **Continue** row by row. Start each row with the offcut of the previous one (≥ 30 cm).
6. **Expansion:** a gap at every wall and around pipes (**holes 20 mm larger than the pipe**) and thresholds. **Expansion joints** at doorways and in big floors (often when a side exceeds ≈ 8–10 m, per the maker).
7. **Check** every 3–4 rows with a straightedge. Don't walk on unlocked edges.

## Element: doors, pipes and thresholds
1. **Door frames:** **undercut** them with a flush saw or an oscillating tool, using an offcut of plank and underlay as the guide, so the floor slides under.
2. **Pipes:** a hole **20 mm larger** than the pipe and a **rosette** over the gap. **Never fix the floor to a pipe.**
3. **Thresholds and transitions:** a **T-profile** where a floating floor meets another floor in a doorway (or in large floors), **reducer or ramp profiles** where heights differ. **Fix the profile's track to the subfloor**, not through the floating floor.
4. **Heights:** check the **door leaf clearance** (≥ ≈ 10 mm above the new floor). **Cut the leaf** if needed and **seal the cut edge**.

## Element: gluing parquet or vinyl
1. **Substrate** primed, flat (≤ 2 mm per 2 m) and at the right moisture. **Dry-lay** the first rows and mark the **starting line** with a chalk line.
2. **Adhesive:** the one the maker approves (elastic MS-polymer or PU for wood, a vinyl adhesive for LVT), the **notched trowel** the sheet names, spread only what you lay within the **open time**.
3. **Lay** the planks into the adhesive, **press with a roller (vinyl) or tap with a block (wood)**, keep the **wall gap** (10–15 mm for wood) and even joints.
4. **Remove adhesive from the surface at once** with the maker's cleaner.
5. **Cure:** walk after ≈ 24 h, heavy furniture after ≈ 3–7 days, **UFH back on gradually** after the adhesive's cure time.

## Element: finishing the floor (cleaning, protection)
1. **Remove spacers**, fit the **skirting** (`carpentry.md`), the **profiles** and the **rosettes**.
2. **Clean** as the maker says (no flooding on laminate).
3. **Protect** the floor with **breathable board or cardboard** during later works (not plastic film). **Felt pads** under furniture.
4. **Keep spare planks** from the same batch.
