# Trade: Pools (outdoor and indoor)

Graph tasks: `pool-plan`, `pool-structure`, `pool-technical`, `pool-heating-and-cover`, `pool-season-care`. Typical values: the **pool maker's drawings**, an **engineer** for any concrete or retaining structure, your country's **pool, electrical and child-safety rules**, and the **permit** win.

**DIY level, honestly:** a pool is a **construction project with drowning, electrical, structural, chemical and groundwater risks**. **DIY realistic for:** above-ground pools, **prefab shell or liner-pool kits** with a contractor doing the excavation and the concrete, the plumbing and filtration installation (water side), landscaping and surrounds. **Hire or require an engineer/licensed pro for:** concrete shells, groundwater control, retaining walls, **all electrical work**, **indoor pools** (building physics, dehumidification), and pool covers over deep water. **A pool is rarely a first project.**

## Types (comparison)
| Type | Pros | Cons | DIY |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Above-ground (steel/polymer panel, inflatable)** | cheap, quick, often no permit | short life, less safe, edge access | ★★★ |
| **In-ground liner pool (panels + liner)** | cheaper than concrete, quick | liner replacement every ≈ 8–12 years, a concrete base is needed | ★★ |
| **Prefab shell (GRP / polymer)** | quick, smooth, low maintenance | crane and delivery access, shell size limited, floating risk | ★★ (with a contractor) |
| **Concrete or block shell (with liner, tile or render)** | any shape, long life | slowest and most expensive, **engineer and builder required** | ★ |
| **Natural swimming pond** | eco, no chlorine | large area, planting, maintenance | ★★ |
| **Indoor pool** | year-round | **specialist** (humidity, ventilation, structure) | ★ |
**Recommendation:** for most households a **prefab shell or liner pool built by a contractor**, with you doing surrounds, landscaping, and the maintenance. **A concrete shell only with an engineer's design.**

## Planning (task: `pool-plan`)
- **Permit and rules:** almost always needed above a volume or depth, plus **fences or barriers** against child drownings, setbacks from the boundary and utilities, and wastewater rules. **Ask the municipality in writing.**
- **Site and ground:** soil type (bearing capacity), **groundwater level** (an emptied pool can **float** or the shell can be pushed out: drainage and a groundwater relief valve), slope (retaining), trees (roots, leaves), sun and wind, distance from the house (noise, humidity), access for an excavator and crane, spoil disposal (a **small 8 × 4 × 1.5 m pool is ≈ 50 m³ of soil, ≈ 90 t**).
- **Utilities:** locate underground lines, **overhead lines**, septic tank and soakaway.
- **Size and depth:** volume = length × width × average depth. Shallow end ≥ 1.0–1.2 m and a deep end ≥ 1.5–2.0 m for swimming. **Depth for diving** needs special design.
- **Technical room:** next to the pool, **flood-proof drainage**, ventilation, room for filter, pump, heater, chemical storage and the electrical cabinet.
- **Running costs:** electricity (pump, heater), chemicals, water, cover, winter.
- **Barrier and cover:** a fence **≈ 1.2–1.5 m** with a **self-closing, self-latching gate** (child-proof, typically opening away from the pool), or a **rated safety cover**, plus an alarm. Check the local rule.

## Structure (task: `pool-structure`)
1. **Excavate** to the drawing (the extra width for working space and backfill). **Slope the sides or shore them** (collapse risk: excavations deeper than ≈ 1.2 m need support or a safe batter).
2. **Base:** compacted gravel and a concrete slab or the maker's base, **perfectly level** (±3–5 mm), with the **main drain** and a **groundwater relief** if needed. Dewater if groundwater is present.
3. **Shell:** set the prefab shell with a crane and level, or build the formwork and reinforcement (engineer), or fit the panels and the liner (wrinkle-free, heated on a warm day). **Penetrations** (skimmer, returns, lights, drains) are fitted and sealed **before** backfill.
4. **Backfill:** gravel or lean concrete in layers **while filling the pool with water at the same rate** (so the shell isn't pushed in), as the maker says. Compact lightly.
5. **Edge, coping and surround:** a stable edge, **falls ≥ 2% away from the pool** to drains, **non-slip surfaces** (R11–R12 outside, class B or C for wet barefoot areas), see `outdoor-surfaces.md` for the details of tiles, paving and decking. Never let water run back into the pool or the technical room.

## Technical (task: `pool-technical`)
- **Filtration:** a sand, cartridge or glass-media filter and a pump sized so the **whole volume turns over in ≈ 4–8 hours** (flow in m³/h ≈ volume ÷ turnover time). **Skimmers:** one per ≈ 25–40 m² of surface, positioned with the wind. **Returns** opposite, a **main drain** with anti-entrapment cover (**entrapment is a drowning risk**).
- **Pipes:** pressure-rated PVC with solvent-welded joints. Slope and isolation valves. **Pressure test** the pipework before backfilling.
- **Heating:** a **heat pump** (best efficiency), a solar collector or a heat exchanger. A **cover** saves ≈ 50–70% of heat loss and evaporation.
- **Electrical (licensed electrician):** the pool rules define **zones around the water**, **RCD 30 mA**, **equipotential bonding** of metal parts, **low-voltage (12 V SELV) lights**, and cable routes in conduit. **Never** DIY.
- **Water treatment:** typical targets: **pH 7.2–7.6**, **free chlorine ≈ 0.5–1.5 mg/l** (a lower figure for outdoor pools with stabiliser), **total alkalinity ≈ 80–120 mg/l**, stabiliser (cyanuric acid) ≈ 30–50 mg/l outdoors, and **shock** as needed. A **test kit or a digital tester** and a log. Salt chlorinators and UV systems are alternatives.
- **Chemical safety:** store **chlorine, acids and algicides separately**, dry, ventilated, **never mix them** (toxic gas), add chemicals to water (not water to chemicals), gloves and goggles, out of children's reach.
- **Winterising:** lower the water level below the skimmers and returns, drain the pipes, fit **winter plugs**, add winterising chemicals, fit a **safety cover**, and **never empty a pool in high groundwater**.
- **Wastewater:** filter backwash and emptying **are not for the sewer or the garden** without the local rules (de-chlorinate, check the permit).

## Outdoor pool specifics
**Siting**
- **Sun:** aim for **≥ 6 hours of direct sun**, with the long side facing the sun. **Wind protection** (a hedge or a wall, not a tall tree close by) reduces heat loss and debris. **Trees** drop leaves, pollen and roots: keep ≥ the tree's height away if you can.
- **Distance** from the house (noise, splash, humidity), **boundary and utility setbacks** per the permit, **a view from the house** (supervision of children), and **a level, stable, well-drained** spot.
- **Overhead lines, drains and septic tanks** can rule a site out.

**Heating and cover (task: `pool-heating-and-cover`)**
- **Uncovered, heated outdoor pools lose a lot of heat,** mostly by **evaporation and at night**. **A cover cuts losses by roughly 50–70%** and keeps leaves out.
- **Heat pump size:** a rough start is **≈ 0.15–0.25 kW of heating output per m³** of water for a covered pool in a temperate climate, then **size from the maker's tables** (volume, climate, target temperature, season). **Solar collectors** (a collector area of ≈ 50–100% of the pool surface) and **solar covers** are cheaper, but slower and weather-dependent.
- **Target temperature** 24–28 °C: **each degree costs about 10–15% more energy.** Check the **panel capacity** (`panel-and-main-services`) and the **noise** of the unit against neighbours.
- **Pump:** a **variable-speed pump** cuts the electricity use by **about half or more** compared with a fixed-speed pump. Run it as long as needed for the turnover, with the filter, at low speed.

**Types for outdoors**
- **Prefab, liner and concrete** as above.
- **Natural swimming pond:** **regeneration (plant) zone typically ≈ 30–50% of the area,** water depth ≥ 1.5–2 m in the swimming zone, a **skimmer and a pump** for circulation, **no chemicals,** and a design by a specialist (the biology is the filter). **Water clarity varies with the season.**
- **Swim spa or a plunge pool:** a small, prefab, counter-current unit (**≈ 5–8 m long, ≈ 8–12 m³**) on a **flat, reinforced slab** (≈ 15–20 cm, per the maker), **electrics by a licensed electrician.** The easiest way to have a heated outdoor pool.

**Winter and seasons (task: `pool-season-care`)**
- **Close** (the water level lowered below the skimmers and returns where the maker says, **pipes drained or filled with antifreeze-safe plugs,** **ice compensators** floated in the water for liner and panel pools, the cover fitted, chemical balance and winterising agent). **Don't empty an in-ground pool** in winter (groundwater, frost).
- **Open** (remove the cover, clean, test the water, restart the system slowly, **re-test the barrier, the gate, the alarm and the cover**).
- **Keep a log** of the chemistry, the filter pressure, and the maintenance.

**Neighbours, rules and insurance**
- **Noise** (pumps and heat pumps), **lighting** at night, **privacy** and **drainage of the emptied or backwashed water** are the usual conflicts. **Tell your insurer** (liability and the barrier requirements).

## Indoor pools (not DIY)
- **Humidity and air:** air temperature ≈ 2 K above the water, relative humidity ≈ 50–65%, a **dehumidification and ventilation system** designed by a specialist, a **vapour-tight envelope**, corrosion-resistant materials, stainless fixings, and condensation-proof windows and doors.
- **Structure:** floor loads, waterproofing, and a plant room. **Chlorine gas and humidity damage ordinary structures.**

## Tools and PPE
Excavator (operator), crane (hire), laser level, plate compactor, concrete tools, PVC pipe cutters and solvent cement, pressure test pump, multimeter (for the electrician), pool test kit, gloves, goggles, respirator for chemical work, safety fencing during the build.

## Common mistakes
- No child barrier or an unlatched gate. **Drowning is silent and fast.**
- Ignoring groundwater: a floating shell or a collapsing liner pool.
- Undersized pump or too few skimmers: algae and stale water.
- DIY electrics.
- Mixing chemicals or storing them next to each other.
- Backfilling without water in the pool.
- A technical room without drainage and ventilation.
- Draining a pool in winter.

## Quality checks
- Level (±3 mm), no leaks (a water loss test over 24 h with evaporation control), pressure tests passed.
- Water chemistry in range, safety barrier and cover tested, electrics inspected and measured.

## Element: excavation and base
1. **Set out** the pool with **string and pegs**, **mark the working space** (≈ 50–100 cm all round) and **the levels**.
2. **Locate** underground utilities and overhead lines. **Plan the spoil removal** (≈ 50 m³ for a small pool, ≈ 90 t).
3. **Excavate** to the drawing. **Excavations deeper than ≈ 1.2 m need a safe batter or shoring.** **Handle groundwater** (pumping, a well-point).
4. **Base:** **compacted gravel and a level slab** (≈ 10–15 cm reinforced concrete, or the maker's base), **level within ±3–5 mm**, **a groundwater relief valve** if the water table is high.
5. **Check** the levels with a laser and **mark the pipes' positions**.

## Element: shell and penetrations
1. **Prefab shell:** **crane**, **centre it**, **level it** with the lifting straps and shims, **don't fill until the maker says**.
2. **Liner or panel pool:** **panels bolted per the instructions**, **braced**, **the liner fitted wrinkle-free** (a warm day), **vacuum out the air** behind it.
3. **Penetrations** (skimmers, returns, drain, lights, steps): **fitted and sealed before backfill**, **the pipe positions photographed**.
4. **Concrete shell:** formwork, reinforcement and pour **by the contractor to the engineer's drawing**, **cured**, **waterproofed** (tile, liner, or render).

## Element: plumbing and technical room
1. **Technical room:** **next to the pool**, **dry, ventilated, with a floor drain**, **space for the filter, pump, heater and chemicals**, **frost-protected**.
2. **Pipework:** **pressure-rated PVC with solvent-welded joints** (the maker's solvent), **falls to the technical room**, **isolation valves**, **a union at every device**, **supports**.
3. **Pressure test** the pipes **before backfill** (the maker's value).
4. **Layout:** **skimmers on the downwind side**, **returns opposite**, **a main drain with an anti-entrapment cover**.

## Element: electrics (licensed)
1. **Electrician only:** **RCD 30 mA**, **equipotential bonding** of all metal parts, **pool zones respected**, **low-voltage (12 V SELV) lights**, **conduit and cable depth** per the rules.
2. **You prepare:** the **conduit routes**, the **boxes in the technical room**, the **photographs**.
3. **Measurements and the report** before filling.

## Element: backfill and edge
1. **Fill the pool with water while backfilling** at the **same rate** (so the shell isn't pushed in), **backfill with gravel or lean concrete in layers**, **compact lightly**.
2. **Edge:** the **coping** (tiles, stone, or a profile) **over a stable bond beam**, **falls ≥ 2% away from the pool**, **non-slip surfaces (R11–R12 outdoors, class B or C for wet barefoot areas)**.
3. **Surround drainage**, so water doesn't run into the pool.

## Element: first fill and start-up
1. **Fill** with clean water (mains or a delivered load), **check levels and leaks**.
2. **Start the pump and the filter**, **bleed the air**, **check the pressure gauge**.
3. **Balance:** **pH 7.2–7.6**, **alkalinity ≈ 80–120 mg/l**, then **sanitiser** (a **shock** if the maker says), **stabiliser outdoors ≈ 30–50 mg/l**.
4. **Run 24–48 h** before swimming, **test again**.
5. **Log** the readings and the settings.

## Element: cover and barrier
1. **Barrier:** a **fence ≈ 1.2–1.5 m** with a **self-closing, self-latching gate opening away from the pool**, or a **safety cover**, **an alarm**.
2. **Cover:** **a safety cover (rated), a solar cover or a thermal roller**. **Know who operates it.**
3. **Test** the gate, the latch and the alarm **at the start of every season** and after storms.
