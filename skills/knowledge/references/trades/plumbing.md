# Trade: Plumbing and drainage (rough-in and sanitary fit-out)

Graph tasks: `plumbing-rough-in`, `sanitary-fit-out`. Typical values: the manufacturer's installation sheet always wins.

**DIY level:** realistic for a careful DIYer **inside the apartment/house** with pressed or compression fittings. **Not DIY / ask first:** work on the building's risers or the main supply, connection to the public sewer, gas boilers, any work on shared parts in apartment blocks (needs the building manager).

## Decide before buying pipe
1. Finished floor level (FFL) and reference line: see graph.
2. **Sanitary items and their installation sheets**: WC frame (wall-hung), basin, tap, shower drain, shower mixer, bath. The product sheet, not this file, gives the exact outlet positions.
3. Shower: tiled walk-in (point or linear drain) vs. tray. Check the drain's **build-up height** against the floor build-up (typically 65–110 mm for linear/point drains, depending on the product).
4. Hot-water supply: distance from the heater (long dead legs = cold water wait and bacterial growth). Consider a circulation line for runs > ~7 m.

## Materials
| Item | Typical choice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Supply pipes | multilayer (PEX-Al-PEX) or PEX or PP-R | one system with matching fittings. Dimension 16 × 2 or 20 × 2 mm for branches, 20/25 for the main bathroom line |
| Fittings | press (rent the press tool) or PP-R welded (rent a welder) | compression/push-fit is easier for DIY. Check that the system is rated for concealed use and ask the manufacturer |
| Insulation | pipe insulation for cold (anti-condensation) and hot (heat loss) | closed-cell, with a vapour barrier on cold water |
| Drain pipes | PP or PE (HT) with rubber seal sockets, sound-insulated types for stacks | WC DN100, shower/bath DN50, basin DN40, washing machine DN50 |
| Brackets and clips | per pipe system | spacing per the data sheet (typically 50–80 cm horizontal for 16–20 mm) |
| Isolation valves | at each fixture group, accessible | angle valves for basin/WC; zone valve for the bathroom |

## Tools
- Pipe cutter, deburring/calibrating tool, tape measure, spirit level (60 cm+), laser
- Press tool (rent) or PP-R welder (rent), or compression spanners (buy)
- Hole saw / core drill for wall and floor penetrations, chisel hammer
- Test pump with pressure gauge (rent, or use a compressed-water test kit)
- Marker, plumb line

## Method: rough-in
1. **Plan on paper**, mark the FFL line and the item positions on the wall. Keep branches short and straight, minimise fittings.
2. **Drains first** (they need the fall): ≥ 1% (≈ 1 cm per m, preferably 1.5–2%) to the stack; no horizontal runs longer than the product allows without a vent; connect the WC at the stack side, not through a basin line.
3. **Floor drain (shower):** set the drain body at the planned height with its flange, using the supplied adhesive or a mortar bed. Level the body with the reference line. **Check the sealing flange height against the screed + waterproofing + adhesive + tile.**
4. **WC frame:** fix to the floor and wall (or the supporting structure) at the FFL reference. Typical bowl rim height 40–43 cm above FFL, seat ≈ 45 cm. Set the flush plate height per the sheet (commonly ≈ 100 cm from FFL to the plate centre, when tiled).
5. **Supply:** run cold and hot at the standard spacing (hot on the left, 15–20 cm apart for mixers, with a wall-mounted shower mixer on an installation plate). Cut square, deburr, insert fitting fully (mark the insertion depth), press once.
6. **Sound insulation:** wrap drain pipes with sound-insulating sleeves/foam, use rubber-lined brackets, separate the WC frame from the walls with sound strips.
7. **Pressure test before closing anything.** Fill with water, bleed air completely, bring to test pressure and hold. Typical: **1.5× working pressure (commonly ≈ 10 bar), held ≥ 30 min with no drop**. Follow the pipe system's own test protocol. Keep the gauge reading in the log. Check every joint with dry paper.
8. **Drain test:** pour water into each outlet, check flow and joints, listen for leaks at the stack.
9. **Photograph** every pipe with a tape measure in the shot before closing, and record the dimensions from corners and the FFL line.

## Method: sanitary fit-out (after grout and silicone planning)
1. Order: **flush plate and WC bowl → basin/furniture → shower fixtures → taps → bath screen/glass**. Don't drill tiles until you check the photos.
2. Drill tiles with a diamond bit **without hammer action**, tape the spot, start at an angle, cool porcelain with water.
3. Use anchors rated for the load. Basin and cabinet loads need backing in drywall (see drywall).
4. Seal with sanitary silicone. **Silicone the wall–floor and wall–wall joints only after** installing items that load the floor/wall (fill the bath while siliconing).
5. Flush the pipes before connecting taps (remove the aerators), clean filters.

## Tips & tricks
- Use a **WC frame with a height-adjustable bracket** if the FFL may change.
- Make the 90° bends in drains with two 45° fittings. They flow better and clog less.
- Don't connect a washing machine and a basin to the same short DN40 line.
- Pressure-test again after the screed or plaster is applied if pipes run in the floor.

## Common mistakes
- Closing the wall before the pressure test.
- Wrong fall: shower drain with fall < 1% or counter-fall.
- Ignoring the build-up height of the shower drain.
- Hot and cold pipes touching: insulate and separate ≥ a few cm.
- Not documenting the pipe positions.

## Quality checks
- Pressure gauge reading stable, no drop (log it).
- Fall ≥ 1% measured with a level and a straight edge.
- WC frame level in both directions, the plate height per sheet ±1–2 mm.
- Isolation valves operate and are accessible.

## Element: drain lines (what to do, in this order)
1. **Find and measure the stack** (position, diameter, access). **Mark the FFL reference** on every wall.
2. **Draw the routes** (WC line DN100 short and straight, shower DN50, basin DN40, bath DN50, washing machine DN50). **Avoid horizontal 90° bends** (use two 45° fittings). **Keep the WC line ≤ ≈ 3 m** and the others short. **Long lines or lines under a lot of depth may need a vent** (an air admittance valve where your rules allow).
3. **Fall:** **≥ 1% (≈ 1 cm per m), preferably 1.5–2%.** Check with a level and a straight edge on the pipe, and **never a counter-fall**.
4. **Assemble from the stack outward** (sockets facing **upstream**), cut square, deburr, **push the pipe fully into the socket and then pull back ≈ 1 cm** (thermal expansion). **Brackets** about every **10 × pipe diameter** (DN50 ≈ 50 cm, DN110 ≈ 1 m), rubber-lined.
5. **Sound insulation:** sleeves around the pipes, **no rigid contact** with the wall.
6. **Fill the lines with water** and look for leaks. **Photograph** every line with a tape measure.
7. **Cap** all open ends (dirt, screed, smell).

## Element: supply lines (cold, hot, insulation)
1. **Plan from the fixtures backwards:** the position of each outlet (WC cistern, basin, shower mixer, bath, washing machine) from the **installation sheets**, with **isolation valves**.
2. **One system and one maker** for pipe and fittings. **Cold below hot** where they run together, **at least a few cm apart**, **insulated** (cold against condensation, hot against heat loss).
3. **Lay out** the lines with **few fittings**, **supports every 50–80 cm** (16–20 mm), **gentle bends**, no kinks. **Mark insertion depths** on the pipe for press and push fittings.
4. **Fix the outlets** (wall plates, elbows) to **backing** at the planned heights (see `sanitary.md`).
5. **Fit the isolation valves** at accessible positions (inside the plate opening, under the basin).
6. **Cap and flush** the lines, then pressure test (next section).

## Element: tests and documentation before closing
1. **Supply lines:** fill with water, **bleed all air**, bring to **test pressure** (typically **1.5× working pressure, commonly ≈ 10 bar**) and **hold for the time the pipe system says** (commonly ≥ 30 minutes) **with no pressure drop**. Check every joint with **dry paper**. **Write down** the date, the pressure and the gauge photo.
2. **Drains:** water test by running each fixture's volume and checking the joints and the flow. **A second person** listens at the stack.
3. **Keep the supply lines under pressure** during screed or plaster work.
4. **Photograph** every pipe and fitting with a **tape measure** and the **FFL reference** in the shot. **Record** the dimensions in the room file.
5. **Only then** close walls and floors.

## Cross-trade
- Waterproofing needs the drain flange + collars for each penetration of the **same system**.
- Pipes in screed: keep them pressurised during pouring.
- Tile layout depends on where outlets land (tap centres should fall on a tile centre or joint cleanly): decide tile format before the rough-in.
