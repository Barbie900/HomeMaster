# Trade: Drywall in wet areas

Graph tasks: `drywall-wet`. Typical values: the **board maker's installation manual** and the tile/waterproofing system data win.

**DIY level:** DIY-friendly. Needs a straight eye, a laser, and patience with joints.

## Which board where?
| Zone | Board | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Showers and splash zones under tile | **cement board** or **foam-core tile backer board (XPS with mesh and cement coat)** | waterproof by itself, but **joints, screws and corners must be sealed** with the system tape and sealant |
| Bathroom walls away from direct water, under tile | **impregnated plasterboard (H2/"green", type H or GM-H)** | needs a **primer + membrane**. It is moisture-resistant, **not waterproof** |
| Bathroom ceiling, painted | **impregnated plasterboard** | moisture-resistant paint, ventilated room |
| Dry rooms | standard plasterboard | |
**Recommendation:** for a walk-in shower: **tile backer board or cement board** on the shower walls, **impregnated board** elsewhere, all covered by the waterproofing system.

## Materials
- **Profiles:** CW 50/75 (studs), UW 50/75 (tracks). Galvanised, with sealing tape under the tracks.
- Boards 12.5 mm (single layer on tile walls; double layer for strength, heavy tiles, and sound).
- Screws: 25 mm for the first layer, 35 mm for the second (for plasterboard), special screws for cement/backer boards (per the maker).
- **Mineral wool 40–60 mm** in the cavity (sound and condensation control).
- Joint tape and filler (**moisture-resistant/tile-grade**), corner beads, primer.
- **Wooden/metal backing (noggins)** for heavy items: basin, wall-hung cabinet, grab bars, towel rail, mirror cabinet, shower seat. WC frame: use the frame's own supports.

## Tools
- Laser or chalk line, level, plumb line, tape measure
- Cordless drill or **drywall screwdriver with depth stop**, bit set
- Plasterboard saw, utility knife, rasp, aviation snips (profiles), metal cutter
- Joint knives 15 and 25 cm, sanding block, mixing paddle
- A pair of **board lifter/props** and a helper for ceiling boards

## Method
1. **Mark** the tracks (floor, ceiling) and stud positions with a laser. Studs at **40 cm centres for single layer under tile** (60 cm if the board's manual allows or for double layer).
2. **Track fixing:** screw or plug every ≈ 60 cm, with **sealing tape** under the profile. Keep the distance from the wet floor: don't let the profile sit in a pool.
3. **Studs:** cut ≈ 1 cm short, twist in, check plumb. Fix the studs alternately.
4. **Services:** run electrical and plumbing through the punched holes with grommets/conduit. **Photograph** before closing.
5. **Backing:** noggins at the heights of the heavy items. Record the positions with photos and measurements.
6. **Insulation** in the cavity (mineral wool) friction-fit.
7. **Boards:** start at the corner. Board edges meet over studs, **joints offset** between layers (≥ 40 cm). Leave a **5–10 mm gap at the floor** (sealed later). Screws every ≈ 25 cm (single layer 20–25 cm; double layer per maker), 10 mm from the edge, **countersink the head just below the surface** (don't tear the paper).
8. **Cut-outs** for boxes with a hole saw. For tap penetrations: cut oversize and seal with the system collar.
9. **Joints:** tape and fill per the system. Wet zones: **sealing tape and sealant** at all joints and corners, **seal every cut edge**, then prime.
10. **Primer** the plasterboard before the membrane or tile adhesive.

## Tips & tricks
- A **tile-backer panel** can be cut with a knife, and the edge can be sealed with the system cement.
- Use **galvanised or stainless fixings** in wet areas.
- Decide the **tile format and the grid before** boxes and profiles: the tile joints and the board joints shouldn't line up in a damaging way.
- Check the board's **permitted tile weight** (kg/m²) in the manual. Heavy porcelain needs double boards.

## Common mistakes
- Treating impregnated board as a waterproof layer. It needs a membrane.
- No backing: the basin or cabinet pulls out of the wall.
- Screws over-driven or too few.
- Not sealing the cut edges in wet zones.
- Profile touching the wet floor without a seal.
- Tile on **thin or flexible** boards: cracking joints.

## Quality checks
- Flatness ≤ 2–3 mm over 2 m. Plumb ≤ 2 mm per 2 m.
- Studs at the planned spacing, boards firmly fixed with no movement when pressed.
- Backing positions photographed.

## Cross-trade
- Plumbing: pressure-tested before closing the wall.
- Electrical: boxes flush with the **final** tile surface.
- Waterproofing: corners and joints sealed, primer used.
- Tiling: board weight rating.

## Dry rooms: partitions, lined walls and suspended ceilings
Graph task: `partitions-and-ceilings`.
- **Partition walls:** UW tracks on the floor and ceiling (with sealing tape), CW studs at **60 cm** (40 cm for heavy loads or tiles), single or double board each side. **Mineral wool 40–60 mm** in the cavity for sound. Bedroom or party walls: double boards and staggered joints. **Doors:** use reinforced studs (UA profiles) or a timber frame.
- **Lined walls (on masonry):** direct-fix boards with adhesive dabs (flat walls only) or a metal frame with an air gap and hangers. **Don't line damp or cold walls without finding the cause** (see `insulation.md`, `basement.md`).
- **Suspended ceilings:** main and cross profiles on hangers every ≈ 100 cm (follow the system tables), boards 12.5 mm screwed every 17–20 cm, **joints offset and staggered**. Leave **service hatches** for valves, junction boxes and the fan.
- **Heavy loads:** TV, shelves, wall units, radiators, grab bars need **backing (noggins) or special fixings**. Use cavity anchors rated for the load only for light items.
- **Finishing:** tape and fill joints (Q2/Q3, see `painting.md`), prime, paint. **Fire and sound:** use fire-rated boards where required (escape routes, boiler rooms, garage links).
- **Tools:** the same as above plus a laser, a ceiling board lift or props, a profile cutter.
- **Mistakes:** boards with all joints in one line, no backing for heavy items, the services closed before the pressure test and the photos.
