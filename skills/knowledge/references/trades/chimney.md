# Trade: Chimney, flue, stove and fireplace

Graph tasks: `chimney-assessment`, `chimney-work`. **Safety-critical.** A bad flue means carbon monoxide poisoning and house fires. The **appliance maker's instructions**, your country's **chimney and fire rules**, and the **sign-off by a licensed chimney sweep or installer** win over everything here.

**DIY level, honestly:** you can **prepare** (hearth, wall protection, wood storage, a CO alarm, tidy access) and **learn to operate** the stove. **Not DIY:** choosing and fitting the flue liner, connecting the appliance, the fire stops, and the commissioning and sign-off. In most countries the **chimney sweep must inspect and approve** a new or changed installation before first use, and sweeps and inspects it regularly after that. **Gas and oil boilers** and their flues are for registered installers only.

## What you must know first
- **Chimney = flue + liner + connection pipe + cap.** The **liner** (clay, stainless steel, or ceramic) is what keeps flue gas inside and the heat away from the structure. An old chimney **without a liner or with a cracked one** is a hazard.
- **Draught** (the chimney pulling) depends on the flue's **height, diameter, insulation and temperature**, and on air supply in the room. A typical stove wants a **draught of about 10–20 Pa**, which the sweep measures.
- **Appliance types:** closed wood stove, open fireplace, pellet stove, masonry heater (tiled stove), and **room-sealed (balanced-flue)** gas or pellet appliances. **Different appliances need different flues** (a condensing boiler needs a condensate-proof flue, a wood stove needs a hot, insulated one).
- **Two chimneys can't be mixed casually.** Don't connect a second appliance to a flue unless a sweep says it's allowed.

## Inspection (task: `chimney-assessment`)
1. **Ask for a sweep's inspection report** (condition, tightness, cleaning, draught, clearances, the cap). It is the first step of any change.
2. **Look yourself (no climbing on the roof):** stains or damp on chimney walls and ceilings, soot or tar marks, loose bricks, a missing or damaged cap, **cracked or crumbling mortar**, an unlined flue, or a flue that doesn't reach above the ridge as it should.
3. **Check around the flue:** timber or insulation too close to the chimney, **no fire stops** at the floor and roof penetrations, cables or pipes running next to it.
4. **Alarms:** a **CO alarm (EN 50291)** at head height or on the ceiling in the room with the appliance and in the sleeping areas, plus smoke alarms. **Test them.**

## Planning the stove or fireplace (task: `chimney-work`)
- **Output (kW) matched to the room.** A typical stove of 4–8 kW heats a 40–100 m² well-insulated space. **An oversized stove overheats the room and burns badly at low output.** Heat loss from the insulation and windows work (`insulation.md`, `windows.md`) should be settled first.
- **Flue diameter** is never smaller than the stove outlet (per the maker, typically Ø130–200 mm). **Effective height** of the flue above the stove (typically about 4–5 m or more) and the **outlet position above the ridge** are set by the rules and the sweep.
- **Liner:** an **insulated stainless-steel liner** (or a ceramic system) is the usual way to line an old chimney. **Condensation** and acids attack unlined brick.
- **Connection pipe (flue pipe)** from the stove to the chimney: short, **rising**, with few bends, **sealed joints**, **cleaning access**, and **clearances to combustibles** as the maker and the sweep specify (often 20–50 cm or more for unprotected pipes, less with insulation).
- **Floor and wall protection:** a **non-combustible hearth** under and in front of the stove (**typically ≈ 50 cm in front and ≈ 30 cm to the sides**, per the rules and the maker), heat shields or a ventilated non-combustible wall behind it.
- **Combustion air:** a closed stove burns roughly **4–5 m³ of air per hour per kW** of output (a 6 kW stove needs about 25–30 m³/h), and the supply must be at least what the maker states. **In airtight homes use an external air connection (pipe from outside)** and a stove approved for it. **A kitchen extractor or ventilation unit can pull flue gas back into the room**, so you need a **safety interlock or a specialist's check** (`ventilation.md`, `kitchen.md`).
- **Fire stops:** where the flue passes floors, the roof or timber, with **non-combustible, insulated collars** from the system.
- **Sign-off:** a **first fire** with the sweep or installer, the **measured draught**, and **a written approval** before regular use. Keep it with the house papers (insurance).

## Operating (the part that is yours)
- **Burn dry wood only.** Moisture **under about 20%** (a moisture meter costs little). Season split logs for 18–24 months under cover. **Wet wood, rubbish, painted or treated wood** make tar, creosote and toxic smoke.
- **Light small, top-down for a cleaner burn**, control air as the maker says, **don't smoulder** with the air closed.
- **Ash:** keep it in a **metal container with a lid, outside, on a non-combustible surface** for at least a few days (embers last).
- **Sweeping:** by the chimney sweep as the rules require (often once or twice a year, depending on use), with the **report kept**. **Chimney fire:** close the air, call the fire service, keep the doors shut, and do not use the chimney again until it has been inspected.
- **CO alarm** batteries and expiry checked. **Never ignore a headache, nausea or dizziness** in the room with the stove.

## Boilers and gas (not DIY)
- Boilers (gas, oil, pellet) are connected, commissioned and inspected by **registered installers**. **Room-sealed** appliances draw air and exhaust through a concentric flue and **don't depend on the room air**, which is the safe choice for airtight homes.

## Tools and materials (for what you do)
Moisture meter for wood, CO and smoke alarms, fire blanket and extinguisher, metal ash bin, hearth tiles or non-combustible board, adhesive and mortar rated for heat, heat-resistant paint, a torch and a mirror for looking into the stove. The flue parts and the installation by the specialist.

## Common mistakes
- Using an old chimney **without a sweep's check**, or without a liner.
- Installing a stove with **insufficient clearance** or without a proper hearth.
- **No external air** in an airtight house, or an extractor hood fighting the stove.
- **Wood that is too wet.** Burning rubbish.
- Disabling or ignoring the **CO alarm**.
- Connecting several appliances to one flue without approval.

## Quality checks
- Written sweep report and approval. **Draught measured.** Clearances checked with a tape.
- CO and smoke alarms tested monthly. No smoke back-spill when lighting or opening the door.

## Cross-trade
- Ventilation and kitchen extractors, insulation and windows (airtightness), roof (`roof.md`: chimney flashing, cap), facade, insurance, region file (the sweep's role and approvals), safety reviewer.

## Element: sweep's inspection and report
1. **Book** a **licensed chimney sweep** (a few weeks ahead in winter). **Tell them** the appliance, the fuel and the plans.
2. **They check:** **cracks, tightness, soot and tar, draught, clearances to timber, the cap and the connection**. They **sweep** and **write a report**.
3. **Prepare:** **access** to the chimney base and the cleaning door, **the room cleared**, **the appliance cold**.
4. **Keep the report** with the house papers (insurance). **Fix every defect** before use.

## Element: liner and connection pipe (installer)
1. **Liner:** an **insulated stainless-steel liner** (or ceramic) **sized to the appliance's outlet**, **lowered from the top** by the installer, **sealed at the top and the bottom**, **a cleaning door**.
2. **Connection pipe:** **short, rising, few bends**, **sealed joints**, **access for cleaning**, **clearances to combustibles** (typically **≥ 20–50 cm for single-wall**, less with insulated pipe, per the maker and the sweep).
3. **Fire stops** at floor, ceiling and roof penetrations, **non-combustible collars**.
4. **Test:** **a smoke or pressure test** by the sweep, **the draught measured**.

## Element: hearth and wall protection
1. **Hearth:** a **non-combustible base** under and in front of the stove (**typically ≈ 50 cm in front, ≈ 30 cm at the sides**, per the maker and the rules): **tiles on cement board**, **stone** or **glass**.
2. **Wall protection:** **a ventilated non-combustible panel** behind and beside the stove at the **distance the maker states**. **Never** fix **plain plasterboard or wood** close to the stove.
3. **Floors:** **a reinforced floor** for a heavy stove (**≈ 100–300 kg**, plus the chimney).
4. **Check** the clearances with a tape.

## Element: combustion air
1. **Airtight homes:** an **external air connection** (a pipe from outside, **Ø100–125 mm**, sealed, **a damper that opens with the stove**).
2. **Kitchen extractors and ventilation units** can **draw flue gas back**: a **safety interlock** or a **specialist's check**.
3. **Check** the **stove's approval** for external air.

## Element: first fire and operation
1. **First fire** only **after the sweep's approval**, **with ventilation**, **in stages** (small fires), **the paint cures and smells**.
2. **Daily:** **dry wood (< 20% moisture)**, **small top-down fires**, **don't smoulder**, **ash in a metal bin outside**.
3. **Sweeping** at the intervals required. **The chimney sweep keeps the log.**
4. **Chimney fire:** **air closed, call the fire service, keep the doors shut**, **no use until inspected**.

## Element: CO and smoke alarms
1. **CO alarm (EN 50291)** in the room with the appliance and in the sleeping areas next to it, **at the height the maker says**, **not near the stove**.
2. **Smoke alarms** on escape routes (`electrical.md`). **Test monthly.**
3. **Symptoms** (headache, nausea, dizziness) near the stove: **ventilate and leave**.
