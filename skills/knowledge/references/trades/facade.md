# Trade: Facade (insulation system, render, repairs)

Graph tasks: `facade-preparation`, `facade-etics`, `facade-render`. Typical values: the **system maker's instructions** (one tested system), your country's **fire and energy rules**, and the **permit** win.

**DIY level, honestly:** a facade is a **scaffold job at height**, heavy (insulation boards, render), weather-dependent, and every detail (corners, windows, plinth) decides whether it lasts. **Subsidies and warranties often require a certified contractor.** Feasible for a careful DIYer on a **single-storey or low building with a rented scaffold and a helper**, or for **render repairs**. **Hire** for multi-storey buildings, mineral-wool systems on tall buildings, or when fire rules are strict.

## Choose the system
| System | What | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ETICS with EPS (white or graphite)** | glued and dowelled boards, mesh-reinforced base coat, finish render | cheapest, light, widespread | combustible (fire barriers on taller buildings), less vapour-open |
| **ETICS with mineral wool** | same, with non-combustible boards | fire-safe, vapour-open, sound | heavier, dearer, more careful fixing |
| **Ventilated cladding (timber, fibre-cement) on battens** | insulation between battens, rain screen on top | robust, repair by panel, vapour-open | needs a frame, detailing at openings |
| **Render only (no insulation)** | repair or new render | cheapest | no energy gain |
**Recommendation:** for most old houses, **ETICS with EPS or mineral wool of the same maker's system**, 10–20 cm, combined with **window replacement in the same project** so the frames sit in the insulation plane. Mineral wool is the safer choice for tall buildings or fire-sensitive sites.

## Before you start (task: `facade-preparation`)
- **Permit and consent:** the façade is often subject to permits, neighbours (boundary overhang), heritage rules, and in apartment blocks **common parts**. Check the region file.
- **Order of works:** **roof and gutters first**, then **windows** (or at least their position), then the facade, so you don't open it twice. Pre-install **window sills, shutters and blind boxes** at the right depth.
- **Substrate:** sound, clean, dry, and flat enough (tolerance of the system, typically ≤ 1 cm per 2 m, otherwise level with the adhesive or a levelling render). **Tap test** for hollow render. **Pull-off test** or the system maker's test for old render and paint. Remove loose render, algae (biocide), flaking paint, efflorescence. **Check for asbestos-cement panels or paint** (never cut or drill).
- **Damp:** find and fix leaks, rising damp and splash zones first. Insulate the **plinth** with moisture-proof insulation (XPS or perimeter boards) **below ground level (≈ 30 cm)** and keep the render clear of the soil.
- **Scaffold:** erected by a competent crew, with edge protection, ties to the wall, access, loading class, and **sheeting** against sun, wind and rain. Check that the scaffold works with the overhang and gutters.
- **Fire rules:** for taller buildings, **non-combustible insulation or mineral-wool fire barriers above windows and at every few storeys**, per local rules and the system approval.

## Materials (all from one system) and quantities (typical)
Starter profile (plinth), adhesive (mortar, ≈ 4–6 kg/m²), insulation boards (+ 5–10% waste), mechanical fixings (plate dowels, typically 6–8 per m², more at corners and on tall facades, length by the substrate), corner and reveal profiles with mesh, drip profiles, window-sill profiles with sealing tape, expansion joint profiles, **reinforcing mesh** (+ 10 cm overlaps) and diagonal mesh strips, **base coat** (≈ 1.3–1.5 kg/m² per mm, typically 3–5 mm ≈ 5–7 kg/m²), primer (≈ 0.2–0.3 kg/m²), **finish render** (grain 1.5–3 mm, ≈ 2.5–4 kg/m²), sealants and tapes, facade paint if required.

## Method: ETICS (task: `facade-etics`)
1. **Level the starter profile** all around (laser). It carries the first row. Fix it per the system with spacers and plugs.
2. **Adhesive** on the boards with the **perimeter-and-dab method** (or full surface with a notched trowel on flat walls), **≥ 40% contact area** on the substrate (more per the system). **Stagger the boards** (running bond), **no cross joints at corners**, and interlock at corners. **No gaps > 2 mm**: fill with foam or insulation strips. **Do not glue into the gaps.**
3. **Reveals, sills and corners:** insulate the window reveals (thinner boards) and **fit the sill profile with sealing tape**. Bring boards flush. The window plane and the sealing tapes come from `windows.md`.
4. **Sand the board joints** flat with a long float, vacuum the dust.
5. **Dowels:** after the adhesive has set (typically 1–2 days), in the pattern and number of the system, **set flush (not crushed)**, with a plate cover.
6. **Base coat and mesh (task: `facade-render`):** apply the base coat to the system thickness, **embed the mesh in the outer third**, **overlap ≥ 10 cm**, put **diagonal mesh strips (≈ 20 × 40 cm) at the corners of every window and door opening**, and corner profiles with their own mesh. Smooth with a trowel. **Cure per the data sheet** (typically about 1 day per mm, at least several days) before the next layer. Protect from sun, wind and rain.
7. **Primer** (tinted), then the **finish render** (rubbed or scraped texture) applied **wet in wet over a whole wall without stopping**, from a natural break or corner to the next. Maintain the wet edge. **Paint** if the system needs it.
8. **Detail:** drip edges, joint tape at the windows, sealant at the gutter brackets, penetrations (cables, lights) with sealing, **expansion joints** where the building has them, **and a gap and a sealed joint between the render and the paving**.

## Conditions
- **+5 to +25 °C** for application and curing, **no rain or frost for 24–48 h or more** (as the maker says), and **no direct sun** on a fresh render: use the sheeted scaffold.
- A facade under a **tight programme** in wet or cold weather fails: plan it for a dry season.

## Render repair only (no insulation)
- Remove all hollow render, **bridge cracks with mesh and a repair mortar**, prime, and re-render with a **breathable, mineral or silicate system** matching the old substrate (don't seal an old wall with a vapour-tight paint).
- Hairline cracks: flexible, breathable crack filler and a fine mesh coat. **Moving cracks** need a structural look.

## Tools and PPE
Scaffold (rent and have it erected), laser, long levels and floats, hot-wire or insulation saw, notched trowels, mixing paddle (heavy duty), mortar mixer (rent), drills for the dowels (SDS), mesh cutters, rubbing float, hawk, trowels, stainless steel trowel, buckets, harness for any work outside the scaffold, helmet, goggles, FFP2 mask, gloves, ear protection.

## Common mistakes
- Opening the facade before the roof and windows are sorted.
- Mixed systems (adhesive, boards and render from different makers).
- Cross joints at corners, gaps in the insulation, boards not flush.
- No diagonal mesh at the windows: cracks at the corners in the first winter.
- Render touching the ground or the paving: the plinth wets and rots.
- Working in rain, frost or direct sun.
- A render stopped half-way across a wall (visible joint).

## Quality checks
- Flatness of the boards (≤ ≈ 3–5 mm under a 2 m straightedge) before the render. Uniform base-coat thickness. No visible mesh.
- Tap test: no hollow sound. No cracks after the first winter.
- Thermography (optional) after the first heating season to find thermal bridges.

## Cross-trade
- Roof overhang and gutters, windows (position, sills, blinds), electrical (outdoor lights, meters), site drainage and plinth, insulation (interior), permits and subsidies, asbestos survey.

## Element: scaffold and protection
1. **Scaffold** erected by a **competent crew**: **edge protection**, **ties to the wall** at the maker's spacing, **access (ladder or stair)**, **a loading class** for the materials, **a board gap ≤ the allowed**, **a sheet** against sun, wind and rain.
2. **Check** every day: **no loose boards**, **no one on an incomplete scaffold**, **a protected route** under it (neighbours, passers-by).
3. **Protect** windows, ground, plants and cars. **Remove** gutters, downpipes, lights, house numbers, **mark the services**.

## Element: substrate repair and plinth
1. **Tap-test** the old render: **cut away hollow** areas, **fix cracks**, **remove algae with biocide**, **wash** the wall with low-pressure water.
2. **Level** big deviations (≥ 1 cm per 2 m) with a **levelling mortar** or **adjust the adhesive thickness**. **Let it dry.**
3. **Plinth:** **XPS or perimeter boards below ground (≈ 30 cm below ground level)**, **waterproofing coat** to the plinth, **a drainage mat** if needed.
4. **Check** the **moisture** in the wall: **dry enough** for the adhesive (the maker's value).

## Element: starter profile and first row
1. **Starter profile:** **level it all round with a laser**, fix with **screws every ≈ 30 cm**, **connectors and spacers** at joints.
2. **Drip edge** and **mesh strip** if the system has them.
3. **First row** of boards sits **in the profile**, **tight, plumb and level**, **no gaps**.

## Element: gluing boards
1. **Adhesive:** the **maker's mortar**, **perimeter-and-dab method** (a bead around the edge, 3 dabs in the middle) or a **full-surface notched trowel** on flat walls. **≥ 40% contact** on the substrate (more per the system).
2. **Boards:** **running bond (staggered)**, **no cross joints at corners**, **interlocked corners**, **joints tight (≤ 2 mm)**, gaps filled with foam or insulation strips (no adhesive in the joints).
3. **Press and level** with a long float, **check flatness every 2–3 rows** with a straightedge.
4. **Reveals:** **thinner insulation**, **continuous with the wall**.

## Element: dowelling and sanding
1. **Wait** until the adhesive has set (typically **1–2 days**).
2. **Dowels:** the **number and pattern** of the system (typically **6–8 per m²**, **more at the corners and on tall walls**), **drill depth and length** by the substrate, **flush, not crushed**, **plate covers**.
3. **Sand** the joints and the surface flat with a **long float**, **vacuum** the dust.
4. **Check** the flatness (≤ 3–5 mm under a 2 m straightedge).

## Element: base coat and mesh
1. **Edge profiles first:** **corner beads with mesh**, **drip profiles**, **window reveal profiles**, **expansion joint profiles**.
2. **Diagonal mesh strips (≈ 20 × 40 cm)** at **every corner of every opening**.
3. **Base coat** to the thickness of the system (typically 3–5 mm), **mesh pressed into the outer third**, **overlap ≥ 10 cm**, **smooth with a trowel**, **no visible mesh**.
4. **Cure** per the sheet (about 1 day per mm, **at least several days**), **protected** from sun, wind and rain.

## Element: reveals, corners and sills
1. **Window reveals:** insulation board, **reveal profile with a sealing lip** that meets the window frame, **base coat and mesh**.
2. **Outer sills:** **sealing tape under the sill**, **fall 3–5°**, **drip edge overhang ≈ 3–4 cm**, **end caps**.
3. **Outside corners:** **corner profiles**, **inside corners:** mesh turned around.
4. **Check** water cannot get behind the sills or the reveals.

## Element: primer and finish render
1. **Primer** (tinted if the render is light and thin), **dry** per the sheet.
2. **Finish render** (typically grain 1.5–3 mm), **apply over a whole wall without stopping** (**wet in wet** from corner to corner), a **team of two** for big walls (one applies, one finishes).
3. **Texture:** rub or scrape with a **plastic float** in **one direction**.
4. **Paint** if the system needs a coat (silicate or silicone-resin paint).
5. **Weather:** +5 to +25 °C, **no rain or frost for 24–48 h**, **sheeted scaffold**.

## Element: details and sealing
1. **Penetrations** (cables, lights, taps): **sleeves** with **sealing tape**, **sealant**.
2. **Gutter and downpipe brackets** with **insulated fixings**, **sealed** around.
3. **Joint at the plinth and the paving:** **a gap and a sealed joint** between the render and the ground.
4. **Final check**: **no gaps, no cracks, all joints sealed**, **the scaffold removed in the dry**.
