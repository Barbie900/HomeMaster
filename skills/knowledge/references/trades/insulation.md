# Trade: Thermal insulation and airtightness

Graph tasks: `insulation`. Typical values: the product data sheets, **the energy rules of your country** and a building-physics check for old walls win.

**DIY level:** attic floor, basement ceiling, and drywall-insulated interior walls are DIY. **Façade insulation (ETICS), interior insulation of old solid walls and roof insulation from the outside** need a permit, scaffolding and often a professional, and subsidies may require certified work. Insulating wrongly causes mould and structural rot, so get the concept checked.

## The two rules
1. **The warm side needs the airtight and vapour-control layer.** Moisture from the room must not get into cold structure and condense there. The layer on the outside should be **more vapour-open** than the one on the inside.
2. **Fix moisture sources before insulating.** Leaks, rising damp and bad drainage can't be insulated away.

## Where it pays most (typical order)
1. **Attic floor or the roof** (largest heat loss, simplest if the attic is unheated and unused).
2. **Windows and external doors** (see `windows.md`).
3. **Basement ceiling** (above an unheated cellar) and the floor.
4. **Exterior walls** (façade ETICS gives the best results, but it's an external project).
5. **Pipes and heat distribution** (cheap and fast): insulate hot-water and heating pipes in unheated spaces.

## Materials (typical thermal conductivity λ in W/mK, lower is better)
| Material | λ | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| EPS (white) | 0.032–0.040 | cheap, façade and floors, moisture tolerant |
| XPS | 0.030–0.036 | load-bearing, below ground and floors |
| Mineral wool | 0.032–0.040 | non-combustible, sound, between rafters and in drywall |
| PIR/PUR | 0.022–0.028 | thin, high performance, check the fire rules |
| Wood fibre | 0.038–0.045 | vapour-open, summer heat protection, higher cost |
| Calcium silicate / mineral boards | 0.05–0.06 | capillary-active interior insulation for old walls |
- **Thickness estimate:** d ≈ λ × (1/U_target − 1/U_existing). Typical U targets for retrofit: roof/top floor ≤ 0.15–0.20, walls ≤ 0.20–0.30, floor over a cold cellar ≤ 0.25–0.35 W/m²K. **Check the actual requirement for your country and subsidies.**

## Method: attic floor (unheated attic)
1. **Seal** the air leaks first (penetrations, hatches, the top of partition walls) with tape, foam or membrane.
2. **Vapour-control layer** on the warm side (on the ceiling below, or on the attic floor). Tape all joints, seal at the walls and penetrations.
3. **Insulate** between and over the joists, with a continuous layer over the joists to cut the thermal bridge. Typical total ≈ 20–30 cm mineral wool or equivalent.
4. **Keep the eaves ventilated** (don't block the air path at the eaves). Don't cover downlights without a rated enclosure.
5. **Hatch:** insulated and gasketed. A boarded walkway on raised supports.

## Method: basement ceiling
1. Fix boards (EPS/mineral wool/wood fibre 8–12 cm) with adhesive and plugs to the underside of the slab. Cover pipes and cables with a plan so they remain accessible.
2. Fire and moisture: a non-combustible layer or cladding where needed, and keep the cellar ventilated and dry.

## Method: interior wall insulation (old solid walls). **Check first.**
- On cold exterior walls the surface gets colder, and **condensation inside the wall** is the risk. Safe options: **capillary-active** systems (calcium silicate, lime or clay-based) or a properly designed system with an airtight, vapour-control layer. Typical thickness is limited (≈ 3–6 cm).
- **Don't use** mineral wool + plasterboard without a vapour control layer on a solid wall, and not on walls that take in rain or rising damp.
- Thermal bridges at floors and partitions remain: plan the return insulation (≈ 50–100 cm) on adjoining walls.

## Drywall-based insulation (new or lined walls)
- Fill the cavity with mineral wool, **friction-fit** with no gaps or compression. Vapour-control layer on the warm side, taped. Don't let the insulation touch cold, damp masonry without a gap or a moisture control.

## Airtightness
- Tape and seal: junctions of wall and roof, window frames, service penetrations, electrical boxes in the vapour-control layer (use airtight boxes). Do an **airtightness check** (smoke pencil, or a blower-door test if you target a certified result).
- **Ventilation:** with tighter envelopes you need a ventilation concept (see `windows.md`, `ventilation.md`).

## Tools and PPE
Knife, long straightedge, staple gun, tape (airtight, butyl or acrylic), sealing foam, drill/driver, plugs, a dust mask FFP2 and gloves for mineral wool, eye protection, a platform, a dust sheet. Don't walk on joists or ceiling boards: use boards.

## Common mistakes
- Insulating before fixing a leak or damp.
- No vapour-control layer on the warm side, or one that's torn or untaped.
- Compressed insulation (loses its value).
- Blocking the roof ventilation path.
- Interior insulation of a damp or rain-soaked solid wall.
- Ignoring thermal bridges at window reveals and slab edges.
- Covering recessed lights, flues or electrical junction boxes.

## Quality checks
- Thermal camera or thermometers on cold days to find gaps. Airtight tape continuous at the checks.
- Moisture readings on timber and walls in the first cold season.

## Cross-trade
- Windows (position), ventilation concept, heating (re-sizing after insulation), electrical (airtight boxes), plaster and paint (reveals), permits and subsidies.
